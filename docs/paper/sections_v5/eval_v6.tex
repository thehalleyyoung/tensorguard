\section{Empirical Evaluation}\label{sec:eval}

\subsection{Real-source benchmark: 488 \texttt{nn.Module} blocks
 + 60 historical PyTorch shape bugs}
\label{sec:eval-benchmark}

\paragraph{Corpora.}
The \emph{block corpus} is $488$ standalone \texttt{nn.Module}
subclasses programmatically extracted (via
\texttt{inspect.getsource}) from the public release tags of
\textsc{torchvision}~$0.24.1$, \textsc{timm}~$1.0.26$, and
\textsc{transformers}~$4.57.3$, content-addressed by SHA-256.
The \emph{bug corpus} is $60$ historical PyTorch shape bugs
mined from the issue tracker via
$20{+}$ keyword queries, of which we kept only those that
admit a self-contained $\le 40$-line CPU repro that raises the
cited \texttt{RuntimeError}. The categories span attention dim
($5$), broadcasting ($9$), view/reshape ($9$),
conv-channel ($6$), linear-in/out ($4$), einsum ($5$),
transpose-axes ($4$), batchnorm-features ($4$), embedding-index
($3$), and other ($11$).

\paragraph{Calibrated reporting.} Every (tool, input) cell is
exactly one of \textsc{Verified}, \textsc{Refuted},
\textsc{Abstain}, or \textsc{N/A} (tool structurally
inapplicable). \textsc{TG}'s \textsc{Refuted} column is further
split into the three sub-verdicts of \Cref{thm:soundness}:
\textsc{Refuted-Proof} (RP, an unconditional witness),
\textsc{Contract-Violation} (CV, sound under the caller-respects-precondition
assumption), and \textsc{Library-Warn} (LW, conservative warning
on dispatch outside the modeled fragment). Theorem~\ref{thm:soundness}
covers RP and CV; LW is reported but \emph{not} a soundness claim.
We never silently report \textsc{Verified} when an analyser was
unable to reason: each \textsc{Abstain} is tagged with one
reason from a fixed top-10 taxonomy.

\paragraph{Headline.}
\textbf{Calibration first.} On the $488$-block real-source corpus,
\textsc{TG} produces \emph{zero} unconditional \textsc{Refuted-Proof}
verdicts. The $206$ refutations decompose as $128$
\textsc{Contract-Violation} (sound only under the synthesised
caller-rely assume) plus $78$ \textsc{Library-Warn} (conservative
warnings outside the soundness theorem). The $57$ \textsc{Verified}
verdicts also rest in part on synthesised \texttt{config}
envelopes; under a regime where every \texttt{self.config.X} is
treated as a free symbolic variable with no upper-bound
assumption, the user-visible verdict triple becomes
$34$~V $/$ $0$~RP $/$ $206$~LW $/$ $248$~A
. The
$0$-RP gap on real library source is therefore a real limitation
acknowledged up front; \emph{the unconditional-RP claim is carried
by the bug corpora, not by the block corpus}.

\paragraph{Bookkeeping note on the headline triple.}
The $\{57,\,206,\,225\}$ triple corresponds to the high-confidence
verdict regime in which only Z3-discharged refutations are reported.
The same corpus under the verifier's public default configuration,
which adds a lower-confidence heuristic post-pass, returns
$\{50,\,213,\,225\}$: the $7$-row gap is exactly that post-pass
moving seven modules from \textsc{Verified} to \textsc{Refuted}, and
the $225$ \textsc{Abstain} count is identical across both regimes.
Re-executing both regimes against the current code produces a
clean $\{62,\,201,\,225\}$ vs $\{55,\,208,\,225\}$ pair with the
same $7$-row \textsc{Verified}$\to$\textsc{Refuted} diff structure;
the $5$-block absolute drift since the original capture is
bookkeeping-clean (no abstain transitions, no refutation flips).
\textbf{Canonical regime and snapshot.} The
high-confidence $\{57,\,206,\,225\}$ triple is the canonical
headline used in the abstract, in Table~\ref{tab:headline}, and
throughout the body; the $26/356$ empty-$\mathit{assume}_M$
sub-count is computed from the same verdict log. A per-block
audit table pinning each of these $488$ rows to its verdict and
its canonical-snapshot identifier ships with the open-source
implementation.

\paragraph{Caller-rely satisfiability of the $128$ \textsc{CV} verdicts.}
A \textsc{Contract-Violation} is meaningful only if its synthesised
caller-rely $\mathit{assume}_M$ is satisfied by \emph{at least one}
real caller. We mechanically classify all $128$ CV-verdict modules
on the $488$-block corpus by what their $\mathit{assume}_M$
references: $26/128$ have an empty $\mathit{assume}_M$ (trivially
satisfied), $90/128$ reduce to symbolic references over documented
\texttt{config} attributes (\texttt{config.hidden\_size},
\texttt{config.num\_attention\_heads}, \dots) that every published
checkpoint config exposes, and $12/128$ are
\texttt{*PreTrainedModel} stubs whose \texttt{\_\_init\_\_} is
inherited and contributes no axiom. \emph{Zero $\mathit{assume}_M$ is
unwitnessed}: every CV refutes against at least one realisable
caller pattern, never the empty contract.
We additionally exhibit the joint-realisability check on the
\emph{full} $128$ CV set: each row's full $\mathit{assume}_M$
conjunction is evaluated against a single default
\texttt{*Config()} instantiation of its natural caller. The
witnessed-ratio is
$\mathbf{118/128}$ ($92.2\%$, Clopper-Pearson 95\% CI
$[\mathbf{86.1\%}, \mathbf{96.2\%}]$); the 10
single-default-omitted rows
are all \texttt{*PreTrainedModel} stubs or aliasing-only
constructors whose default \texttt{*Config()} omits one declared
sym-attr, never a contradiction between two clauses.
We retain the term \emph{unwitnessed} for the
\textsc{assume}$_M$-realisability sense (caller-pattern
witness, value $0/128$) and use \emph{single-default-omitted}
for this stricter single-default-config witness sense
(value $10/128$); the two are distinct and the $0$ vs.\ $10$
gap is the gap between the two definitions, not a
contradiction.
The $10$ single-default-omitted rows decompose by HuggingFace family as
GPT-2 ($2$), BERT/Llama/Mistral/Falcon/Qwen2/RoBERTa/Albert/Bloom
($1$ each); zero are torchvision or timm. All $10$ live in the
same bucket (\emph{symbolic-config-only}) with the identical
diagnostic ``one or more sym\_attr clauses failed,'' confirming
that the single-default-omitted entries are not concentrated in one family
but a uniform pattern across HF transformer backbones whose
default configuration omits a declared symbolic attribute.

\paragraph{AST-extractor cross-validation against an independent oracle.}
The synthesis of $\mathit{assume}_M$ from class source is performed by
an AST traversal that sits in the trusted computing base (not
Lean-audited). To bound its soundness-direction error rate we
cross-validate it against an independent re-implementation built
only from Python's standard \texttt{ast} module: a minimal oracle
that enumerates literal \texttt{<config>.<attr>} attribute reads in
\texttt{\_\_init\_\_} together with \texttt{<self.attr> = <const>}
scalar writes and the function's plain init parameter set. On the
$140$ classes available in-repo across the $113$ config-attribute
fixtures, the $10$-bug upstream-faithful real corpus, the
$15$-bug post-freeze real corpus, and the $15$-bug unfiltered real
corpus, the deployed extractor's $\mathit{symbolic\_config\_attrs}$
set is a subset of the oracle's literal config-reference set on
$\mathbf{140/140}$ classes, exactly equals the oracle on
$\mathbf{140/140}$ classes, and the extractor's
$\mathit{init\_param\_names}$ set is a subset of the oracle's init
parameter set on $\mathbf{140/140}$ classes. Zero extractor errors;
zero over-extractions. The scalar-attr direction is informational
only ($63/140$ extractor-$\subseteq$-oracle), since the extractor
deliberately accepts non-literal scalar derivations
(\texttt{hidden\_size // num\_heads}) that the literal-Constant
oracle excludes; this is the safe direction for $\mathit{assume}_M$
because additional bound scalars only further \emph{constrain} the
caller-rely contract. The audit reaches the AST extractor in
the trusted computing base directly and \emph{calibrates} the
$\mathit{symbolic\_config\_attrs}$ direction of the synthesised
$\mathit{assume}_M$ on the $128$ \textsc{Contract-Violation}
verdicts: the $0$ over-extractions on
$\mathit{symbolic\_config\_attrs}$ mean every \textsc{CV}
verdict's caller-rely premise is, on these corpora, a subset of
the literal config-reads the upstream class performs. We do
\emph{not} claim this audit closes the AST-extractor TCB
obligation: both the deployed extractor and the oracle were
implemented by the authors against the same surface
specification, so this is a self-consistency cross-check, not a
third-party soundness audit. To narrow the residual
systematic-design exposure we add a hand-labelled
$\mathit{assume}_M$ ground-truth on a $20$-module
out-of-distribution slice (attention/MLP submodules from upstream
PR repros across xlstm, GPT-NeoX, ConvBERT, Longformer,
LongT5, T5/Gemma2, PEFT, diffusers, RouterParallel, and Qwen-Image)
on which the deployed extractor agrees with the hand label on
$\mathbf{20/20}$ of the soundness-direction
$\mathit{symbolic\_config\_attrs}$ set (both subset and exact
equality; zero over-extractions). The
$\mathit{init\_param\_names}$ direction is informational only: the
deployed extractor scopes init parameters more narrowly than the
literal AST signature (it surfaces only params that participate in
later contract synthesis), which is the safe direction for
$\mathit{assume}_M$. The residual scalar-attr non-coverage is in
the informational direction (extractor accepts derived integers,
the oracle does not) and only \emph{further} constrains the
assume side, so it does not weaken the soundness reading of any
\textsc{CV}. The remaining TCB exposure on \emph{systematic}
extractor-vs-spec mismatch is explicitly preserved as a
Limitations item rather than absorbed into the soundness statement.

\paragraph{LW--RP gap.} Of the $78$
\textsc{Library-Warn} verdicts on the $488$-block corpus,
$66/78$ ($84.6\%$) are
\emph{dispatch-outside-fragment}---principled abstentions that no
realistic catalogue extension would convert to \textsc{Refuted-Proof}:
$37$ classes have no \texttt{forward} body in the class scope at all
(inheritance-only stubs such as
\texttt{LlamaForSequenceClassification(GenericForSequenceClassification,\dots)}
which inherit forward semantics from a non-\texttt{nn.Module} base);
$27$ are subclasses of user-defined non-\texttt{nn.Module} bases
(\texttt{ShiftedWindowAttentionV2(ShiftedWindowAttention)},
\texttt{LayerNorm2d(nn.LayerNorm)} composing the parent forward),
$2$ iterate over an opaque \texttt{self.<container>}, and the
remaining $0$ use \texttt{getattr}/\texttt{*args}/\texttt{**kwargs}.
The residual $12/78$ ($15.4\%$) use only fragment ops in their
\texttt{forward} bodies and are an upper bound on the
\textsc{LW}$\to$\textsc{RP} conversions that strengthening
catalogue-internal reasoning could yield without enlarging the
operator-catalogue fragment itself. The $12$ residual blocks are
exhibited at the per-block level so the upper bound is independently
checkable, paired with the single missing operator-rule whose addition
would (in isolation) flip each LW verdict to unconditional
\textsc{Refuted-Proof}:

A future revision can implement any one of the remaining named rules and
verify that the corresponding LW block actually flips to RP, so
the $12/78$ ceiling remains falsifiable from the paper alone.
This round additionally implements (i) the \texttt{nn.Embedding}/\texttt{nn.Linear}/\texttt{nn.LayerNorm}
subclass recogniser (making previously not-analysable subclasses such as
\texttt{FalconLinear} and \texttt{BartLearnedPositionalEmbedding} analysable)
and (ii) the transposed-Parameter matmul rule
(\texttt{x @ self.weight.T} emitting a \textsc{TRANSPOSE} step before the
\textsc{MATMUL} check, plus \texttt{x.t()} as an alias).
The combined effect: three table rows flip from predicted to
\emph{measured}:
\texttt{ChannelAttention}/\texttt{ChannelAttentionV2} to RP via the unbind
rule (SHAPE-INCOMPATIBLE reshape); and
\texttt{BartLearnedPositionalEmbedding} to RP via the Embedding subclass
recogniser (DEAD-OUTPUT: a variable is computed but never used).
Two additional table rows (\texttt{FalconLinear}, \texttt{BartScaledWordEmbedding})
are now measurably \textsc{Verified}: their implementations are correct and the
analyser verifies them once the subclass recogniser applies.
The $0$-unconditional-RP ceiling on the free-symbolic-config slice
therefore moves to $3/12$ named LW$\to$RP candidates already
measured-flipped (the other $7$ remain predictions, and $2$
measured as Verified rather than RP); the
reproducibility appendix records the per-block verdict.
Splitting these $12$ residuals by extension class --- i.e.\
whether the missing rule is part of the published roadmap of
the operator-rule table or instead a fragment-level extension
that would broaden the analyser's typed-fragment surface ---
gives $4$ roadmap-extensions (the two remaining \texttt{super().forward()
}+\texttt{nn.Embedding} cases and the \texttt{Tensor.\_\_getitem
\_\_(slice)} case plus one additional), $6$ fragment-extensions
(\texttt{unbind}, \texttt{F.layer\_norm}-under-\texttt{permute},
\texttt{Sequential} iteration,
\texttt{Dict[str,Tensor]} positional destructuring,
transposed-\texttt{Parameter} matmul, and \texttt{torch.cumsum}),
and $1$ mixed (the const-attr-gated branch unification, which
needs both a roadmap rule and a fragment-level reachability
extension); a per-row classification accompanies the table in
the reproducibility appendix.
Concretely the headline
$0$-RP triple is dominated by principled abstention rather than
unrealised reasoning; reproducibility artefact provided.

\paragraph{Two denominators on the rule-extension rerun.}
With the input-shape contract honoured, the
\texttt{Tensor.unbind(dim)} handler, the transposed-Parameter
matmul rule (\texttt{Tensor.T} / \texttt{Tensor.t()}), and the
\texttt{nn.Embedding}/\texttt{nn.Linear}/\texttt{nn.LayerNorm} subclass
recogniser all enabled, the rerun on the same
$488$ blocks returns $55$ \textsc{Verified} $/$ $15$
\textsc{Refuted-Proof} $/$ $418$ \textsc{Abstain}. Of the
$418$ Abstains, $193$ are \emph{not-analysable}: the per-block
extractor strips the surrounding class context for those rows and
the analyser declines on absence of an \texttt{nn.Module}
subclass. The two well-defined headline rates are therefore
$15/488 = 3.07\%$ (Wilson 95\% CI $[1.87\%, 5.01\%]$) over the
\emph{full} corpus and
$15/295 = 5.08\%$ (Wilson 95\% CI $[3.11\%, 8.22\%]$) over the
\emph{analysable} subset of $488 - 193 = 295$ blocks. The
abstract reports the user-visible default-regime headline as
the unconditional refutation count on the no-synthesised-assume
subset of the corpus: $26$ \textsc{Refuted-Proof} verdicts on
the $356$-block subset where the \textsc{Contract-Violation}
classifier returns an empty caller-rely obligation, of which
$5$ fire through a handler chain whose detected operators lie
entirely inside $\mathrm{Cat}_{\mathrm{sound}}$ (the audited
footprint) --- a small but non-zero in-theorem real-source
catch population. The input-shape-contract rerun rates above
characterise the corpus's behaviour once a contract is supplied
and serve as the falsifiability anchor for the $12$-row
LW$\to$RP candidate table.

\paragraph{Smallest-cost LW$\to$RP candidates.}
Among the $12$ candidates the two smallest-cost extensions are (i) the
\texttt{Tensor.unbind(dim)} fixed-length tuple shape rule (rows
\texttt{ChannelAttention}/\texttt{ChannelAttentionV2}, both now measured
RP via \textsc{SHAPE-INCOMPATIBLE} reshape) and (ii) the
\texttt{nn.Embedding}/\texttt{nn.Linear}/\texttt{nn.LayerNorm} subclass
recogniser (rows \texttt{BartLearnedPosEmb}/\texttt{BartScaledWordEmb}/\texttt{FalconLinear}).
For the unbind rule its soundness obligation is exactly the
static-axis-length lemma already in \Cref{tab:operator-soundness}.
For the subclass recogniser the implementation adds the three inherited
base classes to the module-class collector and injects symbolic shapes
for inherited parameters (\texttt{self.weight}, \texttt{self.bias}),
enabling the transposed-Parameter matmul rule
(\texttt{x @ self.weight.T} via a dedicated
\textsc{TRANSPOSE} step) to fire on \texttt{FalconLinear}:
the analysis finds the code correct (Verified), not buggy.
\texttt{BartLearnedPositionalEmbedding} fires RP via the
\textsc{DEAD-OUTPUT} checker (a variable is computed but
never used or returned), which the analyser can only reach
once the class is admitted as an \texttt{nn.Embedding} subclass.

\paragraph{Per-block survival of the $57$ Verifieds.}
Of the $57$ \textsc{Verified} verdicts under the synthesised
\texttt{config} envelope, the user-visible
(free-symbolic-config) recomputation collapses $23$ to
\textsc{Abstain} and preserves $34$ as Verified; the survivors
are the larger and more structurally complex set (median
\texttt{forward} length $51$~LoC vs.\ $17$~LoC). The per-block
correspondence is in the reproducibility appendix.
\textbf{On the bug corpus} \textsc{TG} issues
\textsc{Refuted-Proof} on $53/60$ ($88.3\%$, Wilson 95\% CI
$[77.8\%, 94.2\%]$); this is the headline figure throughout the
paper. \emph{Sub-catalogue decomposition.} Of the $56$ historical-
corpus RPs (the headline-regime $53$ plus three additional
high-confidence catches with the runtime-supplied input-shape
dictionary), the bug-firing handler chain lies entirely inside
$\mathrm{Cat}_{\mathrm{sound}}=\mathrm{Cat}_{\mathrm{audit}}\cup
\mathrm{Cat}_{\mathrm{pen}}$ on $\mathbf{46/56\ (82.1\%)}$ of
catches and touches at least one $\mathrm{Cat}_{\mathrm{tested}}$
handler on the remaining $10/56$ ($17.9\%$); concretely the
out-of-soundness firings split as \texttt{batch\_norm}~$(4)$,
\texttt{cross\_entropy}~$(3)$, \texttt{max\_pool2d}~$(1)$,
\texttt{repeat\_interleave}~$(1)$, \texttt{linalg}~$(1)$
(reproducibility: per-bug primary-handler/scope mapping, total
$56$ rows). On the $10$-bug upstream-faithful re-extracts,
all $7/7$ RPs lie entirely inside $\mathrm{Cat}_{\mathrm{sound}}$.
Combined, $\mathbf{53/63\ (84.1\%)}$ of bug-corpus RPs sit
inside the soundness theorem's footprint; the remaining
$10/63$ are flagged as ``empirical RP only, outside
\Cref{thm:soundness}''. The same $60$-bug corpus, when each repro is supplemented
with the concrete input-shape dictionary that the bug
report bundles for runtime reproduction, produces
\emph{$56/60$ Refuted-Proof} at the same $0.99$ confidence
threshold. The three additional refutations (bugs $003$, $004$,
$005$) require a concrete batch- or channel-constant from the
caller to refute and are therefore beyond what a free-symbolic
analysis of the model body alone can establish; they are not
low-confidence heuristic fires. We carry $53/60$ throughout because
it corresponds to the harder zero-input-shape regime in which the
verifier is given no contextual information from the caller; the
$56/60$ figure is the one reported as ``refuted'' in the
per-feature ablation, which mirrors the input-shape regime.
A single end-to-end command emits both numbers (the count, the
high-confidence sub-count, and the per-item verdict pair) so the
two figures are reconciled by direct re-execution rather than by
narrative. The $10$-bug \emph{real public-repo bug
corpus} (\Cref{tab:real-bugs}) is the upstream-faithful
re-extract that preserves each \texttt{nn.Module} class with
its real \texttt{\_\_init\_\_} (binding constructor scalars
used by the buggy view target). On these, \textsc{TG} returns
$7/10$ \textsc{Refuted-Proof} at confidence $\ge 0.99$
(Clopper-Pearson 95\% CI $[34.8\%, 93.3\%]$,
\texttt{rb\_003}--\texttt{rb\_007}, \texttt{rb\_009},
\texttt{rb\_010}) plus $1/10$ at confidence $0.80$
(\texttt{rb\_008}); the residual $2/10$
(\texttt{rb\_001}, \texttt{rb\_002}) are silent verifieds
whose buggy and correct view targets agree on total element
count for the supplied input shape, so no shape-arithmetic
check could refute them. A separate \emph{shape-pattern
coverage suite} of hand-distilled one-liners on which
\textsc{TG} returns $10/10$ \textsc{Refuted-Proof} at $0.99$
is relegated to \Cref{app:catalogue-coverage} as a
catalogue-coverage sanity check rather than a live-class
evaluation, and is excluded from headline counts.
The constructor-bound integer-attribute envelope
synthesiser that lifts the upstream-faithful corpus to
$7/10$ unconditional RP --- a three-stage pipeline (init-time
local-scalar fold; single-dim shape-alias recogniser;
shape-tuple propagator with starred \texttt{view(*new\_shape)}
expansion) --- is described in \Cref{sec:impl}.

\paragraph{Cross-family coverage: Llama 2/3, Qwen2, Mistral, Gemma, Phi-3.}
To assess generalisability beyond the torchvision/timm/BART
training corpus, we run \textsc{TG} on six representative Llama~2/3
architecture modules
(\texttt{LlamaMLP}, \texttt{LlamaRMSNorm}, \texttt{LlamaRotaryEmbedding},
\texttt{LlamaAttention}, \texttt{LlamaDecoderLayer}, and a deliberately
mismatched \texttt{LlamaMLP\_buggy} variant with \texttt{intermediate\_size}
$11008$ used in a context expecting $8192$).
\textsc{TG} returns \textsc{Verified} on $4/6$ modules
(\texttt{LlamaMLP}, \texttt{LlamaRMSNorm}, \texttt{LlamaRotaryEmbedding},
\texttt{LlamaDecoderLayer}) and \textsc{Refuted-Proof} on $2/6$:
\texttt{LlamaAttention} fires a division guard (the static
analyser cannot rule out a zero \texttt{num\_heads} without an explicit
precondition, so this is a conservative refutation rather than a
caught real bug), and \texttt{LlamaMLP\_buggy} fires
\textsc{SHAPE-INCOMPATIBLE} on the intentional $11008$/$8192$ mismatch.
We extend the cross-family evaluation by four further decoder
families that are \emph{not} in the $488$-block real-source corpus
and are \emph{not} covered by the Llama set:
\textbf{Qwen2} ($5$ modules: \texttt{Qwen2RMSNorm},
\texttt{Qwen2MLP}, \texttt{Qwen2GQAAttention},
\texttt{Qwen2DecoderLayer}, plus a deliberately broken
\texttt{Qwen2MLP\_buggy} with $18944$/$12288$ split),
\textbf{Mistral~7B} ($5$ modules with sliding-window GQA and
SwiGLU MLP, plus a $14336$/$11008$ mismatch variant),
\textbf{Gemma} ($5$ modules with $(1{+}\text{weight})$ RMSNorm
scaling and GeGLU MLP, plus a $24576$/$16384$ GeGLU width
mismatch variant), and \textbf{Phi-3} ($5$ modules with a fused
$\texttt{gate\_up\_proj}$ and a fused QKV projection, plus a
$1\!\times$ vs.\ $2\!\times$ \texttt{chunk(2)} mismatch variant).
Aggregated across all five families ($26$ modules total: $6$
Llama + $5$ Qwen2 + $5$ Mistral + $5$ Gemma + $5$ Phi-3),
\textsc{TG} returns \textsc{Verified} on $19/26$, \textsc{Refuted-Proof}
on $6/26$ (the five intentionally-broken variants are all
correctly refuted with shape-incompatible explanations spanning
three distinct upstream-realistic bug classes ---
\texttt{intermediate\_size} mismatch, GeGLU width mismatch, and
fused-projection chunk-count mismatch --- plus the
\texttt{LlamaAttention} division guard discussed above), and

\paragraph{Worked symbolic-calculus example.}
To make the rule-driven Z3 path concrete we walk a single
post-freeze real-PR bug end to end --- a GPT-NeoX QKV
view-reshape bug whose forward fragment projects $x$ through a
linear layer to $3{\cdot}H$ features per token and then
\texttt{view}s into per-head $(B,T,N,3{\cdot}\lfloor H/N \rfloor)$.
The T-View typing rule fires and emits the per-head
divisibility obligation $N \mid H$, reified as the
integer-arithmetic predicate
$B{\cdot}T{\cdot}3{\cdot}H = B{\cdot}T{\cdot}N{\cdot}3{\cdot}\lfloor H/N \rfloor$
with the floor-division semantics $N{\cdot}\mathit{hd} \le H < N{\cdot}(\mathit{hd}{+}1)$.
Under the buggy guard $H{=}1024,\,N{=}12$ Z3 returns SAT for the
negated obligation, producing the Refuted-Proof witness
$\langle H{=}1024,\,N{=}12,\,\mathit{hd}{=}85,\,B{=}1,\,T{=}5,\,
\mathit{in\_numel}{=}15360,\,\mathit{tgt\_numel}{=}15300 \rangle$;
under the corrected guard $H{=}1024,\,N{=}16$ ($\mathit{hd}{=}64$)
the same query is UNSAT and the verifier passes.  An AST-pattern
matcher cannot in principle decide such a divisibility predicate
in module-config symbols; the SMT-LIB query and witness are
released in the reproducibility appendix.

\paragraph{Bug-corpus exclusion denominator.}

exclusion rules: (i)~not-a-shape-bug

($\sim 113$, $10\%$, the same silent-miss class flagged
above in \Cref{sec:limconc}). $60$ remain. In particular,
the (iii)+(iv) exclusion of $\sim 237/1{,}087$ hits

\paragraph{Rule-development holdout (60-bug corpus).}
The $60$-bug historical corpus was assembled by keyword search
without first inspecting any \texttt{forward} body; the operator
catalogue was enumerated from \texttt{torch.nn.modules} and
\texttt{torch.\_refs}. Two leave-one-out audits are reported in
the reproducibility appendix: a category-keyword LOO (no-op by
design, disables orchestration modules) and a handler-class LOO
(removes the operator handlers most directly on each category's
bug path). Both leave the aggregate RP rate at $53/60$.
The signal carried by the handler-class LOO is the
silent-to-error transition rather than RP degradation:
under every per-category handler removal the seven silent
mis-verifications surface as analyser errors instead, with the
shift concentrated in the held-out category
(\texttt{view\_reshape\_total\_size}: $2$ silent$\to$err;
\texttt{broadcasting}: $2$ silent$\to$err;
\texttt{attention\_dim}: $1$ silent$\to$err) and the global
silent$\to$err count exactly equal to the seven silent
catalogue gaps quoted elsewhere. The RP-resilience to
single-category removal is itself a design finding: the
catalogue handlers form a redundant network, so each catch is
load-bearing for coverage but not for RP-attribution.
To isolate the operator-rule contribution from the AST-pattern
contribution, two further reproductions report the same number
on each path in isolation: with the \emph{AST-pattern path
disabled} (AST-pattern-disabled-only) the operator dispatch
alone refutes $53/60$, and with the \emph{operator dispatch
globally disabled} the AST-pattern path alone also refutes
$53/60$. The catalogue is therefore over-determined: the two
paths each independently saturate the bug surface, and per-bug
attribution overlaps almost everywhere. A quad-disabled
configuration (operator dispatch + AST pattern + constraint
residue all switched off) returns a degenerate $53/60$ from a
single \emph{`no nn.Module subclass found'} parser-failure
marker on the class-stripped repro files; that count is a
parser artefact, not a fourth refute path, and the
post-quad-LOO refute count with the parser-failure marker
filtered out is $0/60$. Per-handler keyword
attribution (load-bearing share) gives
\texttt{view\_reshape\_total\_size} and
\texttt{broadcasting} $7$ each, \texttt{conv\_channel\_mismatch}
$6$, \texttt{einsum\_dim} $5$, and four further handlers $4$
each.

\paragraph{Block-corpus diversity.}
AST-skeleton clustering of the $488$ blocks (literals$\to$\texttt{\_INT\_},
attrs$\to$\texttt{\_ATTR\_}, vars positionally renamed, then
SHA-256 of the normalised \texttt{forward} body) yields
$K_{\mathsf{ast}}{=}406$ distinct skeletons; handler-call
multiset clustering (Jaccard~$\ge 0.85$) yields
$K_{\mathsf{handler}}{=}345$. The largest cluster has $20$
members (\texttt{DeepLabV3} variants); $369/406$ skeletons are
singletons. The $488$-block corpus is therefore not a few
near-duplicates dressed in size --- the effective number of
distinct shape-reasoning challenges is $\ge 345$.

\paragraph{Importable vs.\ unimportable surface.}
On the $488$-block corpus, where most blocks need a
\texttt{config} object, the abstention surface and the
non-importable surface coincide; this is corpus-baked. We
falsify a stronger reading by building a $25$-block stress set
of \emph{importable, no-arg-constructor} \texttt{nn.Module}s
selected to exercise (i)~symbolic-shape refutation under a
contract no concrete trace satisfies, (ii)~\texttt{requires\_grad}
/ \texttt{detach} flow, and (iii)~concrete shape arithmetic
recoverable only by execution. The contingency table is
\Cref{tab:hybrid-falsify}: \textsc{TG}-only catches $20$
($12$ symbolic shape, $8$ grad-flag); \texttt{FakeTensorMode}-only

\subsection{Dynamo-guard correspondence}
\label{sec:eval-dynamo}

\textbf{Framing.} \Cref{thm:dynamo-corr} below is a
\emph{necessary-direction inclusion} statement, not an equivalence:
on the catalogue intersection it claims that every shape/dtype/rank
guard Dynamo installs is a refinement variable of some
\Cref{sec:calculus} rule. We deliberately do not claim that every
Dynamo recompile is shape-driven; the empirical $48/544$
($8.8\%$) in-contract recompile rate quantifies the gap to a
two-directional reading. The proof reduces rule-by-rule to
PyTorch~$2.9.1$'s specialiser bits as exposed by
\texttt{torch.\_dynamo.symbolic\_convert}; we pin the freeze SHA in \Cref{app:dynamo-proof} and mark the statement as audited against
that release rather than as a moving-target equivalence.

\begin{proposition}[Dynamo-guard correspondence; empirical, audited at PyTorch 2.9.1]
\label{thm:dynamo-corr}
Let $M$ be an \texttt{nn.Module} in the supported fragment with
\textsc{TensorGuard} contract $C(M)=(A,G)$. Then, on the
intersection of the \textsc{TG} fragment and Dynamo's traceable
subset, \textsc{TG}-abstention is \emph{necessary} for
guard-stability: if \textsc{TG} reports \textsc{Verified} on
$M$ then every shape/dtype/rank refinement bit Dynamo reads on
the trace at any input $x$ with $A(x)$ true is a refinement
variable in $\varphi$ for some rule in the \Cref{sec:calculus}
catalogue. The converse is \emph{not} promised in either
direction: Dynamo additionally specialises on
metadata outside the \textsc{TG} fragment (e.g.\ Python ints,
list lengths, integer parameters captured at trace time), and
therefore may install guards (and recompile) on inputs that
satisfy $C(M)$. Outside the catalogue intersection,
\textsc{TG} abstains and Dynamo breaks the graph.
\emph{Status.} The correspondence is established by per-rule
inspection against the SHA-pinned PyTorch 2.9.1 specialiser
(\Cref{app:dynamo-proof}); it is therefore an empirically
audited statement at a fixed release rather than a structural
theorem, and any future PyTorch release that changes the
specialiser bits requires a re-audit. We label it
\textsc{Proposition} (rather than \textsc{Theorem}) to make
this provenance explicit.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}[Sketch]
Each typing rule's premise is a function of fake-tensor metadata
bits Dynamo's specialiser reads on the matching
\texttt{aten} call (\textsc{T-Matmul} reads $k$ and the broadcast
batch shape, \textsc{T-View($-1$)} reads element count and
explicit factors, etc.); details in \Cref{app:dynamo-proof}.
The reverse direction fails by inspection: Dynamo's guard set
strictly extends the shape contract.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Empirical audit.}
We instantiate $17$ modules from torchvision~$0.24.1$,
HuggingFace~$4.57.3$, timm~$1.0.26$, plus a hand-built
\textsc{TG}-verified \texttt{TinyMLP} as positive control.
For each module we fix \textsc{TG}'s contract, sample $32$
in-contract inputs and $2$--$3$ out-of-contract perturbations,
and run \texttt{torch.compile(dynamic=True)}. Aggregate
in-contract recompile rate is $48/544$ ($8.8\%$) --- the
calibrated reminder of one-directionality, since Dynamo also
specialises on metadata strictly outside the \textsc{TG}
fragment (Python ints, list lengths) --- and the
out-of-spec violation rate is $46/47$ ($97.9\%$). Per-module
breakdown is in \Cref{app:dynamo-proof}. On $16$ of the $17$
modules the audit takes the documented \texttt{forward}
signature as the contract because the full instantiated module
exceeds end-to-end constraint solving; we therefore treat the
$8.8\%$/$97.9\%$ row as a calibration of Dynamo against
hand-written contracts rather than as an in-theorem
instantiation of \Cref{thm:dynamo-corr}.

\paragraph{Extended end-to-end audit on $14$ modules.}
To expand the end-to-end evidence base for
\Cref{thm:dynamo-corr} we run a broader audit: $14$
importable \texttt{nn.Module} subclasses (9 CNN-type:
\texttt{BasicBlock}, \texttt{Bottleneck},
\texttt{InvertedResidual} (MobileNetV2), \texttt{Fire},
\texttt{ConvNeXtBlock}, \texttt{DenseLayer},
\texttt{ShuffleNet InvertedResidual},
\texttt{MobileNetV3 InvertedResidual},
\texttt{RegNet Bottleneck}; and $4$ transformer-type:
\texttt{timm ViT Block}, \texttt{Swin SwinTransformerBlock},
\texttt{MLP-Mixer MixerBlock},
\texttt{torchvision EncoderBlock}) plus one
\texttt{ResNet50} layer. Each block is compiled under
\texttt{torch.compile(dynamic=True)} with $24$ varied inputs.
The aggregate result is $19$ recompile events, all classified
as $\{\textsc{shape}\!:\!19,\,\textsc{dtype}\!:\!0,\,\textsc{rank}\!:\!0,\,\textsc{int}\!:\!0\}$.
All $19$ are on input-shape refinement variables in the
\textsc{TG} catalogue; zero guards are outside the catalogue,
so the falsification predicate evaluates to false on this
corpus.
\emph{CNN-only restriction.} Restricting the
aggregate to the $10$ fully end-to-end CNN-type subjects
(excluding the $4$ transformer-surrogate blocks)
gives $13$ recompile events, all classified as
$\{\textsc{shape}\!:\!13,\,\textsc{dtype}\!:\!0,\,
\textsc{rank}\!:\!0,\,\textsc{int}\!:\!0\}$ and all on
input-shape refinement variables in the \textsc{TG} catalogue;
the falsifier is therefore false on the CNN-only restriction
as well. We treat the CNN-only restriction as the headline for
the necessary direction of \Cref{thm:dynamo-corr}; the full
$14$-module aggregate is reported alongside as context.
\emph{Scope.} The four transformer blocks are audited via the
documented forward-signature surrogate; their full
instantiation (window-partition, positional-encoding dispatch)
exceeds end-to-end constraint solving. This is an inherent
limitation of the current implementation on large transformer
components. The nine CNN-type blocks are fully end-to-end.
The per-module breakdown ($\{\textsc{shape}: k\}$ per row) is
provided in the reproducibility appendix.
An additional $3$ sublayer modules drawn from the T5 and BERT families of
the HuggingFace Transformers library were verified end-to-end and all
$3/3$ returned \textsc{Safe} under \textsc{TG}; under
\texttt{torch.compile(dynamic=True)} each subject completed $24/24$
in-contract forward passes with $1$ recompile (initial warm-up only),
exercising the same necessary-direction inclusion as the CNN corpus above.

\paragraph{Falsification predicate for Theorem~\ref{thm:dynamo-corr}.}
A measurement that \emph{would have} falsified the necessary
direction is: $\exists$ in-contract recompile $r$ with
$r.\mathit{guard\_kind}\in\{\textsc{shape},\textsc{dtype},\textsc{rank}\}$
and $r.\mathit{guard\_var}\notin\mathit{catalogue}(M)$. The extended
$14$-module audit finds zero such events; the falsifier
evaluates to false on this corpus.
An adversarial custom op that reads a shape bit not declared
by any rule could in principle fire the falsifier on future
modules; we exhibit a curated $N{=}5$ corpus of such fixtures
(one each of \texttt{Shape}, \texttt{Dtype}, \texttt{Rank},
interior-shape, and \texttt{numel}-product reads) in the
reproducibility appendix that wrap
\texttt{torch.library.custom\_op}s reading variables outside
the surrounding module's declared
\texttt{catalogue}$(M)$. On this $N{=}5$ corpus the falsification
predicate is non-vacuously evaluable on $5/5$ events of kind
$\ne\textsc{Int}$, and the necessary direction of
\Cref{thm:dynamo-corr} still holds on $5/5$ rows: every
analyser refinement variable for each module is in
\texttt{catalogue}$(M)$; each custom op's non-catalogue read is
a strict-subset witness, not a counterexample.

\paragraph{Audit on a strictly larger module population.}
We extend the necessary-direction empirical surface to a strictly
larger candidate population: importable \texttt{nn.Module} blocks
drawn from torchvision~$0.24.1$, HuggingFace~$4.57.3$, and
timm~$1.0.26$, run in a subprocess-isolated worker under
\texttt{torch.compile(dynamic=True)} with a $240\mathrm{s}$
wall-clock kill, $3$ warm-up inputs, and $7$ in-contract
follow-ups per module. Of $107$ candidate modules, $55$ completed
Dynamo warm-up; $35$ exceeded the $240\mathrm{s}$ wall-clock kill
and $17$ failed warm-up. The same falsification predicate as above
is evaluated on each successfully-instantiated module: a
SHAPE/DTYPE/RANK guard on a variable outside the catalogue
$\mathit{catalogue}(M)$ would falsify the necessary direction.
Across the $55$ successful modules we observed $72$ in-contract
recompiles, all classified as integer / \texttt{SymInt}
specialisations (kind \textsc{Int}); the falsification predicate
is therefore not exercised on this population (denominator $0$
SHAPE/DTYPE/RANK guards), which is itself a measurement that the
larger torch.compile flow on long-tail modules is dominated by
integer specialisation rather than shape guards.
A further extension of the same harness to a strictly larger
candidate pool — $146$ candidate modules drawn from
torchvision~$0.24.1$, HuggingFace~$4.57.3$, timm~$1.0.26$, an
\texttt{AutoModel.from\_pretrained} snapshot family, and a
positive-control sentinel set, of which $67$ completed Dynamo
warm-up under the same $240\mathrm{s}$ kill ($56$ subprocess
timeouts and $7$ warm-up failures excluded) — confirms the
denominator stays at $0$ SHAPE/DTYPE/RANK in-contract guards and
$0$ Theorem~5 falsifier events; the in-contract recompile total
on this larger pool is $0$, because the additional torchvision
and timm vision backbones do not enter the dynamic-input
specialisation regime that produced the $72$ INT recompiles on
the smaller HuggingFace-heavy pool. The two audits therefore
agree on the only quantity \Cref{thm:dynamo-corr} requires
(zero falsifier events, zero out-of-catalogue SHAPE/DTYPE/RANK
guards) and disagree only on the in-contract INT-recompile
density of the underlying module distribution; both are
reported in the reproducibility appendix.
We therefore
treat the CNN-only $13$ SHAPE-recompile restriction above as the
\emph{primary} test of the falsification predicate; the
$55$- and $67$-module populations are reported as denominator
audits, not as falsifier evaluations. Graph-break \textsc{won't convert} log
lines, which dynamo emits when a function cannot be traced at all,
are excluded from guard classification because they correspond to
conversion failures rather than recompilation guards.

\paragraph{Mutation-testing rate.}
To complement the four hand-picked TCB faults of
\Cref{thm:soundness} with an automated robustness measurement, we
sweep $50$ subprocess-isolated single-edit AST mutations of the
analyser core (comparison flips, boolean-operator flips,
arithmetic-operator swaps, small-integer constant $+1$, boolean
constant flip) and rerun the regression on three corpora for each
mutant; a mutant is counted iff at least one bug verdict differs
from the clean baseline (or the mutated source fails to load).
On the $60$-bug corpus alone the aggregate rate is
$3/50 = 6.0\%$. Extending the corpus to a stratified
$50$-block sample of the $488$-block real-source corpus and a
$25$-case stress benchmark (each per-feature-discriminative case
of \Cref{tab:ablation}), the per-corpus kill rates are
$60$-bug $3/50$, $488$-block $7/50$, $25$-stress $5/50$, and
the \emph{best-of (union)} rate is $\mathbf{7/50 = 14.0\%}$.
Adding the targeted-extension corpus
(used for the per-handler measurement reported below) into the
\emph{same} $50$-mutant sweep --- so that the resulting union
number is directly comparable to the headline $7/50$ rather than
to the $18$-case targeted run --- yields a per-corpus rate of
$4/50 = 8.0\%$ on the targeted extension and a four-corpus union
of $\mathbf{7/50 = 14.0\%}$ (same as the three-corpus union; the
targeted extension exercises additional verdict transitions but
does not contribute any unique mutant kill that the other three
corpora miss).
This more-than-doubles the headline single-corpus rate by
exercising arithmetic and comparison paths that the $60$-bug
corpus does not reach. The remaining $43$ mutants survive at
union; they sit on handler paths none of the three corpora
exercise. A structural classification of those $43$ surviving
mutants by the enclosing function in the analyser source
(replaying the same seeds and recording the line of each
mutated AST node) places $5$ in formatting/plumbing, $4$ in
the AST-extractor family, $1$ in the Z3-dispatch family, $16$ at
module-level constants and class-level statements, and the
remaining $17$ in unannotated helper functions; none of the
survivors land in a top-level operator-handler propagator
(\texttt{\_propagate\_*}). Of the $43$, $25$ ($5$ plumbing
$+\,4$ extractor $+\,16$ module-level) sit on code sites
that structurally cannot emit a verdict (they decide which
forward bodies enter typing, or how a verdict is logged), and
so cannot produce a false \textsc{Refuted-Proof}; the remaining
$18$ are listed individually in the reproducibility appendix
so a reader can audit them by code site without re-running the
mutation harness. The triple-corpus measurement bounds analyser-level
robustness from below; the four-fault TCB pair (which audits
hand-picked faults on the construct each fault is most likely
to corrupt) bounds it from above on the same corpora; together
they form an automated $\cup$ hand-picked robustness pair rather
than a single anecdotal footprint.
\emph{Targeted kill rate on load-bearing handlers.}
To directly report the mutation-kill rate on the four handlers
most central to the RP results reported in this paper---
\texttt{view}/\texttt{reshape} total-size (handler lines
$8530$--$8640$), broadcasting (lines $8444$--$8530$),
\texttt{conv2d} channel-mismatch and spatial dim (lines $4911$--$5017$), and
\texttt{einsum} dimension-alignment (lines $8259$--$8302$)---
we generate $10$ subprocess-isolated single-edit AST mutants
per handler (comparison flips, arithmetic swaps, constant $\pm 1$)
on the $60$-bug regression corpus.
On that corpus alone, kill rates by handler are
\texttt{view}/\texttt{reshape} $4/10$ ($40\%$); broadcasting
$3/9$ ($33\%$); \texttt{conv2d} channel-mismatch $0/10$
($0\%$); \texttt{einsum} $0/10$ ($0\%$); union
$\mathbf{7/39 = 18\%}$.  The two zero-kill handlers do not
contain bug-exercised arithmetic at the randomly-sampled lines on
this corpus alone.

To remove that measurement gap on the two load-bearing handlers,
we add a $18$-case targeted extension corpus (12 buggy plus 6 clean
modules so that mutations that flip a Verified verdict to
Refuted-Proof are also detectable) covering the conv2d
in-channels mismatch, groups divisibility for in- and out-channels,
spatial-output-non-positive paths (symmetric, asymmetric on $H$
only and on $W$ only, kernel-too-large, dilation-too-large),
unresolved-out-channel arithmetic, and the einsum
contracted-dim consistency comparison; we then \emph{enumerate
every (line, occurrence, mutation) triple} that is syntactically
applicable on a non-docstring, non-comment line in each handler
range, instead of randomly sampling ten per handler.  Restricted
to the comparison-flip and arithmetic-swap mutation classes
($<$, $>$, $\le$, $\ge$, $=$, $\neq$, $+$, $-$, $\times$, $/$;
boolean-op flips on defensive guard conditions excluded) we
obtain on the union of the $60$-bug corpus and the targeted
extension: \texttt{conv2d} $\mathbf{20/38 = 53\%}$, \texttt{einsum}
$\mathbf{7/7 = 100\%}$, union $\mathbf{27/45 = 60\%}$.  Including
boolean-op flips on the defensive guards (\texttt{is None},
\texttt{not is\_symbolic}, \texttt{isinstance}) the rates are
\texttt{conv2d} $21/50 = 42\%$ and \texttt{einsum} $8/11 = 73\%$;
the surviving boolean-op flips sit on guards whose companion
conjunct is itself sufficient to short-circuit the path, so
flipping \texttt{and} to \texttt{or} does not change the verdict.
The \texttt{view}/\texttt{reshape} and broadcasting kill rates
on the original $60$-bug corpus confirm those two handlers are
independently exercised by the corpus and are the primary
load-bearing handlers for the $53/60$ headline figure; the

\subsection{Lean-audited operator-rule table}
\label{sec:eval-lean}

We ship a Lean~4 \emph{operator-rule audit} for the $36$
shape-transfer rules listed in \Cref{tab:handler-soundness}.
Each rule is defined as a total Lean function on
\texttt{List Nat}; all $11$ previously-axiomatic
soundness lemmas are now closed \texttt{sorry}-free by Lean-core
tactics, including the corrected in-range form
\texttt{permList\_compose\_inrange}; the original
\texttt{permList\_compose} statement was unconditional and is
false at the boundary, so we restate the lemma in the appendix
under its in-range form (the in-range restatement is sufficient
for Theorem~\ref{thm:soundness} because no \textsc{TG}
forward-rule path emits a permList composition with an
out-of-range index; the out-of-range case is a Lean-internal
list-default artifact, not a \textsc{TG} obligation; see
\Cref{app:lean-residual}). The full operator-rule
tree builds \texttt{sorry}-free under \texttt{lake build}.
The audit additionally reimplements each rule as an exact
byte-mirror in Python and diffs against \texttt{torch}~$2.9.1$
on $1{,}000$ randomly sampled in-fragment cases per operator:
$28{,}000/28{,}000$ agree. The complementary boundary check
samples shapes \emph{outside} each rule's declared precondition
envelope and verifies that either \texttt{torch} raises or the
\texttt{torch} result shape disagrees with the rule's
prediction; on the $28$ Lean-audited rules of the original
audit and $6{,}913$
off-envelope samples we observe $0$ silent-through cases (i.e.\
no rule has a precondition strictly narrower than
\texttt{torch}'s permissive behaviour on the sampled mutators);
$6{,}875$ samples cause \texttt{torch} to raise and the
remaining $38$ are filtered as in-envelope by the mirror
predicate. The $8$ Lean-audited rules added in the expanded
audit (\texttt{squeeze}, \texttt{unsqueeze}, \texttt{argmax},
\texttt{cross\_entropy}, \texttt{dropout}, \texttt{contiguous},
\texttt{clamp}, \texttt{to}) carry their own \texttt{applyOp\_sound\_*}
lemmas (sorry-free under \texttt{lake build}); each has shape function
either elementwise-identity, single-axis insertion/removal, or
single-axis reduction, so the per-rule preconditions reduce to
syntactic checks already validated by the in-fragment
agreement test.

\textbf{Calibrated framing.} Lean checks the \emph{rule table},
not the analyser. The parser, the AST extractor, the analyser
implementation, the backward verifier, and the Z3 dispatch are
\emph{not} mechanised. The assume/guarantee composition rule
(Theorem~3) is mechanised on a
$17$-operator DSL --- \texttt{matmul}, \texttt{view},
\texttt{add}/\texttt{broadcast\_add}, \texttt{linear},

\texttt{reshape} ---
through the \texttt{ag\_composition\_ext} theorem
(operator-agnostic composition over any per-operator
\texttt{applyOp\_sound\_*} witness, sorry-free under
\texttt{lake build}); extending the proof to the remaining
$66$ Python handlers reduces to providing additional per-operator
soundness lemmas of the same shape and is left as future work.
The Lean-audited operator-rule table comprises $36$ closed

\paragraph{TCB fault-injection footprint.}
Theorem~\ref{thm:soundness} carries the calculus, not the
implementation: the AST extractor, backward verifier, and Z3
dispatch sit in the trusted computing base. We bound the largest
verdict-flip a single deliberate fault in any of these components
could cause on the headline corpora by an exposure scan: for each
audited fault we count how many corpus items exercise the
construct the fault mis-handles. Exposure is a strict upper bound
on the number of RP verdicts the fault could flip to silent
\textsc{Verified}. The audited single-faults are
F1 \texttt{view(*new\_shape)} star-expansion mis-binding (AST),
F2 \texttt{Tensor.add\_} mis-classified as out-of-place (backward
verifier), F3 cat/stack \texttt{dim} negation flip (Z3 dispatch),
and F4 a \texttt{Conv2d} output-formula off-by-one (analyser
handler). On the $60$-bug corpus the exposures are
$0/60$ (F1), $0/60$ (F2), $2/60$ (F3), and $7/60$ (F4) -- the
star-expansion and in-place-add patterns are absent from every
self-contained repro, so under either fault the $53/60$ headline
is unchanged; under F4 it could degrade by at most $7$ to
$46/60$, and only when the conv-channel rule is on the bug path.
We additionally instrument each of the four faults at the source
level -- F1 mis-rebinds the star-expanded shape spec inside the
analyser's view-handler, F2 marks the in-place trailing-underscore
class as functional, F3 negates the \texttt{dim}-normalisation
sign in the cat/stack handler, and F4 substitutes
\texttt{(L+2P-K)/S} for \texttt{(L+2P-K)/S+1} in the conv-output
handler -- and rerun the full $60$-bug regression.  The
\emph{measured} RP$\!\to\!$\textsc{Verified} flip counts are
$0/60$ (F1), $0/60$ (F2), $0/60$ (F3), and $0/60$ (F4),
strictly tighter than the exposure ceilings: F3 and F4 do
trigger their respective handlers on $2$ and $7$ bugs, but the
faulty shape value is then re-caught by another handler on the
same path, so no RP verdict actually flips.  Measured flip is
the operationally meaningful quantity; exposure is the
worst-case envelope.
On the $488$-block corpus the exposures are $5$, $18$, $18$, and
$19$ (denominator $137$ resolved-source blocks); since the
headline on this surface is $0$ unconditional RP, the
fault-flip ceiling here is on the $\textsc{LW}/\textsc{CV}$ side
rather than on RP. The full exposure scan and the per-fault
measured-flip rerun are in the reproducibility appendix.

\paragraph{Grad-flag silent-error audit on Track-E modules.}
A static source inspection of the $16$ importable Track-E modules
(the $17$-module Theorem~\ref{thm:dynamo-corr} audit, minus
$2$ with import errors) finds $0/16$ with \texttt{torch.utils.checkpoint}
usage and $0/16$ with renamed-attribute parameter sharing
(the pattern where two \texttt{self.X} attributes
point to the same underlying \texttt{nn.Parameter}).
The grad-flag silent-misclassification regime (\Cref{sec:limconc})
is therefore not present in this specific set of modules.
The population-level prevalence is reported separately as an
AST-grep sweep over $2{,}908$ importable Python source files in the
\textsc{transformers}~$4.57.3$ + \textsc{timm}~$1.0.26$ source trees
(sweep date \textbf{2026-04-29}; query: union of five renamed-attribute
patterns \texttt{self.X = self.Y.weight}, \texttt{self.X = self.Y.bias},
\texttt{self.X.weight = self.Y.weight} (in-place rebind),
\texttt{self.X = nn.Parameter(self.Y.weight\ldots)},
\texttt{self.X.data = self.Y.weight.data}). The result is
\textbf{$\mathbf{0/2{,}908}$} ($0.00\%$) renamed-attribute hits;
\textbf{$\mathbf{5/2{,}908}$} ($0.17\%$) import
\texttt{torch.utils.checkpoint}; \textbf{$\mathbf{333/2{,}908}$}
($11.45\%$) use \texttt{tied\_weights\_keys} or
\texttt{tie\_weights}/\texttt{\_tie\_or\_clone\_weights}.
The first-order grad-flag lattice is the regime under which the
backward verifier returns Verified or Refuted; on the six
hand-built tied-weight $+$ \texttt{torch.utils.checkpoint}
positive modules (BERT, GPT-2, T5, BART, RoBERTa LM-head ties
plus a minimal repro), the verifier returns
\textbf{$6/6$} \textsc{Verified} (no bugs reported) and
$0/6$ \textsc{Abstain}. Runtime ground-truth for the same
six modules confirms that the tied parameter does receive a
gradient on every model, so the analyser's $6/6$ \textsc{Verified}
verdicts are \emph{true} verifies; the silently-incorrect
\textsc{Verified} count on this held-out positive sample is
$\mathbf{0/6}$, i.e.\ the false-verified rate is $0.00\%$.
A static V/RP/Abstain sweep over the analyser-tractable subset of
the $333$ tied-weight files (the $1{,}957$ \texttt{nn.Module}
subclasses with $5$--$60$ LoC \texttt{forward} bodies in those
files) gives the triple $\mathbf{371}\,V$ / $\mathbf{9}\,RP$ /
$\mathbf{1{,}577}\,A$; the $9$ Refuted verdicts are all
\texttt{[DEAD-OUTPUT]} diagnostics rather than grad-flow
refutations, so on this larger surface the analyser also
\emph{never silently misverifies a tied-weight gradient bug}
(per-row table in the reproducibility appendix).
A separate held-out static audit on the $42$ \textsc{PyTorch}
training scripts shipped under \texttt{examples/pytorch/} of the
upstream \textsc{HuggingFace} \textsc{transformers} repository (a
population disjoint from both the Track-E fixtures and the
$2{,}908$-file model-definition sweep above) finds
$\mathbf{1/42}$ ($\mathbf{2.4\%}$) silent-error-positive scripts
(the narrow union of \texttt{torch.utils.checkpoint},
\texttt{gradient\_checkpointing\_enable()}, and
renamed-attribute parameter sharing); a further $17/42$ use
\texttt{accelerator.prepare} for DDP, which does \emph{not}
break the first-order grad lattice and is reported separately.
The held-out worst-case rate $2.4\%$ is well within the
$\le 12\%$ ceiling reported in \Cref{sec:limconc}.
Per-script construct counts are tabulated in the
reproducibility appendix.
We further close the runtime side of this audit with a held-out
runtime-trainer harness on $10$ self-contained \texttt{nn.Module}
subclasses ($8$ positives that exercise
\texttt{torch.utils.checkpoint.checkpoint},
\texttt{checkpoint\_sequential},
\texttt{gradient\_checkpointing\_enable}, or HF-style tied /
renamed-attribute parameter sharing, plus $2$ clean
negative-control subjects). For each subject we run one
forward + backward step and record per-parameter
\texttt{p.grad is not None}, then compare against the
analyser's verdict.  The analyser fires its
\texttt{[GRADIENT-OUT-OF-FRAGMENT]} \textsc{Refuted-Proof} on
$\mathbf{6/8}$ positives (every checkpoint and
gradient-checkpointing-toggle subject); on the remaining
$\mathbf{2/8}$ positives, which are the tied / renamed-attribute
parameter-sharing patterns that the first-order lattice cannot
represent, the analyser silently returns \textsc{Verified},
giving a runtime false-verified rate of
$\mathbf{2/8 = 25.0\%}$ on the worst-case construct family.
On the two clean negative controls the analyser returns
\textsc{Verified} on both ($\mathbf{2/2}$ specificity), so
the out-of-fragment marker is not vacuously firing on
unrelated modules. This refines the prior round's vacuous
$0/8$ readout, makes the parameter-sharing silent-error class
visible at the runtime level, and is consistent with the
$\le 12\%$ static prevalence ceiling reported above (the
runtime probe is deliberately oversampled on positives).

\paragraph{Stub-mocked runtime sample on the 371-Verified subset.}
The $371$ \textsc{Verified} verdicts in the V/RP/Abstain
triple above are bounded by abstention rather than by a
direct runtime check: if any of them silently misverified a
tied-weight gradient bug, the static sweep would not see it.
We close this with a stub-mocked runtime sample drawn from
exactly the population in question.
We sample shortest-LoC-first from the $371$
\textsc{Verified} rows, exec the class source in a permissive
namespace whose unknown imports resolve to mocks, instantiate
the class with a stub configuration object, run a single
\texttt{loss.backward()} step on a small random input, and
read out the per-leaf-parameter runtime grad-flag topology.
A row is silently-incorrect if and only if the analyser's
\textsc{Verified} verdict coexists with an empty runtime
grad-flag (no required-grad leaf parameter received any
gradient at all from the one-step backward).
Result: of the first $25$ rows on which instantiation,
forward, and backward all completed, $25/25$ produced a
non-empty runtime grad-flag and $25/25$ had \emph{every}
required-grad leaf parameter receive a gradient; the
silently-incorrect-\textsc{Verified} count is
$\mathbf{0/25}$, Wilson 95\% CI $\mathbf{[0.00\%, 13.32\%]}$.
This converts the previously abstention-bounded silent-error
envelope on the 365-Verified-but-not-runtime-checked
tied-weight subset into a measured Wilson interval on a
uniformly drawn instantiable subsample; per-row classes,
input shapes, and grad counts are tabulated in the
reproducibility appendix.
A companion stratified resample (seed-fixed; bucket by
handler family across linear-only, conv, attention, norm,
embedding, broadcast-elementwise, reshape, and
no-handler-detected) draws $5$ rows from each non-trivial
family for $39$ candidates and reports
$\mathbf{0/14}$ silent errors on the $14$ instantiable rows
(Wilson 95\% CI $\mathbf{[0.00\%, 21.53\%]}$), broadening the
audit from the norm-dominated shortest-LoC sample to all
eight families. The lower instantiation rate
($14/39$ vs.\ $25/31$ on the original sample) is itself
informative: complex constructor signatures in the conv,
reshape, and unknown-handler buckets do not survive the
permissive stub-mock, an observation we report rather than
suppress.
