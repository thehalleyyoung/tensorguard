\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

Tensor programs in PyTorch fail in two characteristic ways that
unit tests rarely catch: \emph{(i)} the forward pass crashes deep
inside an autograd-traced subgraph because two operand shapes
disagree under a particular batch size, sequence length, or
image resolution; and \emph{(ii)} the backward pass silently
produces a wrong gradient --- e.g.\ a parameter receives
\texttt{p.grad=None} after \texttt{loss.backward()} because an
in-place op or a \verb|.detach()| broke the autograd tape along
one branch, and \texttt{optimizer.step()} silently skips it.

The PyTorch ecosystem responds to these failures with
\emph{execution-based} tools: \texttt{torch.fx}+ShapeProp
captures one trace, \texttt{FakeTensorMode} executes shape
arithmetic on metadata-only tensors, \texttt{torch.export}
specialises a trace, and \texttt{TorchDynamo} installs runtime
guards that recompile when an invariant fails. All four require
the model to be importable, instantiable, and traceable on the
target machine. On modern HuggingFace/timm code that is
typically \emph{not} the case: a transformer block needs a
\texttt{config} object, an attention head reads
\texttt{self.config.hidden\_size}, and an
\texttt{nn.ModuleList} over \texttt{config.num\_layers} blocks
demands a fully-realised configuration before a single tensor
can flow.

\textsc{TensorGuard} takes the opposite stance. Given an
\texttt{nn.Module} \emph{class source}, with no instantiation
and no tracer, it computes a refinement-typed signature
$\mathsf{Tensor}\{s,g\mid\varphi\}$ for every value flowing
through \texttt{forward}, where $s$ is a symbolic shape, $g$ is
a static gradient-required flag, and $\varphi$ is a
Z3-decidable refinement constraining both. The
\textsc{Abstain} verdict is reported as a first-class outcome
whenever a subgraph falls outside the verified fragment, so the
analyser never silently claims verification on constructs it
cannot reason about. A separately scoped exploratory result
(\Cref{thm:dynamo-corr}) gives a one-directional inclusion
relating these refinement variables to the metadata reads that
\textsc{TorchDynamo} issues on the matching \texttt{aten} call;
the inclusion is necessary-direction only, audited on $17$
modules, and is not claimed as a runtime equivalence.

\paragraph{Contributions.}\hangindent=1em
\textbf{(C1)} A refinement-typed calculus
$\mathsf{Tensor}\{s,g\mid\varphi\}$ that unifies symbolic shape
and static gradient-flag analysis under one Z3 procedure
(\Cref{sec:calculus}), with classical
Preservation/Progress (\Cref{thm:preservation,thm:progress}).
The calculus itself reorganises constraint-based shape
typing rather than inventing it; the distinctive substance
is its joint shape-plus-grad layer (C3), its un-instantiated
class-source regime described above, and the audit and
calibration package (C5--C6) that partitions every verdict
by handler-soundness scope.
\textbf{(C2)} An assume/guarantee discipline at the
\texttt{nn.Module} class boundary, with a contravariant/covariant
subclassing rule (\Cref{sec:assume-guarantee}); the soundness of the
composition rule (\texttt{ag\_composition\_ext}) is mechanised in
Lean~$4$ on a $17$-operator DSL (\texttt{matmul}, \texttt{view},
\texttt{add}/\texttt{broadcast\_add}, \texttt{linear},
\texttt{transpose}, \texttt{permute}, \texttt{relu},
\texttt{cat}, \texttt{sum\_reduce}, \texttt{mean\_reduce},
\texttt{expand}, \texttt{gather}, \texttt{embedding},
\texttt{conv2d}, \texttt{einsum}, \texttt{unbind}, \texttt{reshape});
the composition theorem is operator-agnostic over any per-operator
\texttt{applyOp\_sound\_*} witness, so the four operators that fire
on the post-freeze real-PR catches
(\texttt{view}/\texttt{reshape} as a non-trivial shape rule,
\texttt{conv2d}, \texttt{einsum}, plus \texttt{unbind}) are now
\emph{inside} the mechanised composition fragment with their own
soundness lemmas (\texttt{applyOpExt\_sound\_view},
\texttt{applyOpExt\_sound\_reshape},
\texttt{applyOpExt\_sound\_conv2d},
\texttt{applyOpExt\_sound\_einsum},
\texttt{applyOpExt\_sound\_unbind}, all sorry-free under
\texttt{lake build}). The algorithmic discipline is implemented
over the full $79$-handler catalogue; the remaining $62$ handlers
sit under per-rule lemmas or under tested-only soundness scope, as
\Cref{sec:limconc} discusses.
\textbf{(C3)} An autograd-aware backward verifier catching
three canonical silent-zero-grad bug classes statically
(\Cref{sec:impl-backward}); $500/500$ static$\leftrightarrow$runtime
agreement, $8/8$ canonical bugs caught, $0/50$ false positives.
The lattice is first-order
($\{\texttt{has\_grad},\texttt{no\_grad},\top\}$) and is silently
incorrect on parameter-sharing-under-renamed-attribute (prevalence
$\le 12\%$ of training scripts; \Cref{sec:limconc}); we flag this as
a substantive caveat, not a footnote.
\textbf{(C4)} An \emph{exploratory} \emph{Dynamo-guard inclusion lemma}
(\Cref{thm:dynamo-corr}, necessary direction only): every
\textsc{TG} refinement variable is, on the matching
\texttt{aten} call, a metadata read \textsc{TorchDynamo}'s
specialiser issues on a \texttt{FakeTensor}. The mapping is
shipped as data ($28$ rows; mechanically rechecked
against the imported torch --- $28/28$ pass under \texttt{torch~2.9.1}). The lemma is
empirically audited on a $17$-module corpus drawn from
\texttt{torchvision}, \texttt{transformers}, and \texttt{timm}; on
$16$ of the $17$ modules the documented forward-signature contract
(equivalent to what TG would emit after CEGAR closure) is used as
the assumed contract, and the remaining row is a TG-verified
surrogate. We accordingly
scope C4 as an \emph{exploratory} result.  We do \emph{not} claim a
two-directional correspondence.
\textbf{(C5)} A $488$-block + $60$-bug benchmark with calibrated reporting under a five-way verdict
taxonomy per tool (\Cref{sec:eval-benchmark});
a per-feature stress benchmark on $25$ targeted cases
(\Cref{tab:ablation}) reporting that, on the
hand-designed $25$-case stress benchmark, only three knobs ---
\textbf{device-consistency}, \textbf{gradient-flow}, and
\textbf{low-confidence gating} --- move verdicts (the
corresponding ablation on the real corpora is a flat line, with
none of the five knobs flipping a verdict;
\Cref{sec:eval-benchmark}); a $25$-block hybrid-mode falsification corpus
(\Cref{tab:hybrid-falsify}); and an AST-skeleton diversity
analysis ($K_{\mathsf{ast}}{=}406$ on the $488$ blocks).
The contribution is restricted to those three discriminative
knobs; the unused CEGAR loop and the always-satisfiable
phase encoder ship with the analyser but are not claimed as
contributions.
The localisation tracer is reported as engineering rather
than as a research result.
\textbf{(C6)} A Lean~4 \emph{operator-rule audit} of the
\textbf{shape-transfer rule table}: $28$
shape-transfer rules with $11/11$ previously-axiomatic
soundness lemmas closed sorry-free, the full
operator-rule tree building \texttt{sorry}-free
under \texttt{lake build}, and
$28{,}000/28{,}000$ in-envelope samples agreeing with
\texttt{torch~2.9.1} (\Cref{sec:eval-lean};
\Cref{tab:handler-soundness}). \emph{The rule table is
audited; the analyser implementation, AST extractor, and
backward verifier are \emph{not} audited.} On the $488$-block
real-source corpus, $33/57$ \textsc{Verified} verdicts and
$95/128$ \textsc{Contract-Violation} verdicts touch \emph{only}
handlers in the Lean-or-pen-paper audited footprint
($128/185$ in-soundness verdicts in total, $69.2\%$); the
remaining verdicts split into $12/185$ touching at least one
of the $34$ tested-only handlers and $45/185$ touching only
handlers outside any of the three soundness scopes (a TCB
obligation explicitly tracked, \Cref{sec:eval-lean}).
