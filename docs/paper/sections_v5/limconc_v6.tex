\section{Limitations and Conclusion}\label{sec:limconc}

\paragraph{Scope.} We analyse the typed AST fragment
$\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{TG}}$ (\Cref{sec:fragment}):
\texttt{nn.Module} classes with statically resolvable layer
constructors, straight-line \texttt{forward} (with
shape-deterministic \texttt{if}$^\dagger$/\texttt{for}$^\dagger$),
and the operator catalogue of \Cref{app:ops-extended}.
Decorator metaprogramming, runtime attribute mutation, custom
\texttt{autograd.Function} subclasses, and truly data-dependent
control flow are out-of-scope and trigger sound
\textsc{Abstain} --- the same intersection on which the
necessary direction of \Cref{thm:dynamo-corr} holds. The
backward verifier tracks grad refinements and severed-tape
conditions; it does not verify numerical correctness or
second-order derivatives. The soundness theorem
(\Cref{thm:soundness}) covers the \textsc{RP} and \textsc{CV}
verdicts only; \textsc{LW} is a conservative warning.
Lean does not mechanise the AST extractor, the analyser
implementation, or the backward verifier; the assume/guarantee
composition rule is mechanised on the $17$-operator
\texttt{ag\_composition\_ext} fragment, and the boundary for the
remaining $62$ handlers is shown in
\Cref{tab:handler-soundness}.

\paragraph{Semantically-aliased view bugs.}
A residual class of real upstream bugs is undetectable by any
purely shape-arithmetic check: the buggy and the (upstream-fix)
correct view targets agree on total element count for the
input shape that exercises the bug, and the symptom is a
silently-wrong tensor permutation rather than a runtime
\texttt{RuntimeError}. On the upstream-faithful re-extraction of
the $10$-bug real-public corpus (\Cref{tab:real-bugs},
$2/10$ silent verifieds on \texttt{rb\_001}, \texttt{rb\_002})
this is the precise residual failure mode: the input shape
$(2, 4, 2{\cdot}64, 192)$ has the same product as both
\texttt{view(2,4,2,64,192)} (buggy) and any equivalent
$5$-dim factoring whose trailing dim divides $192$. On these two
inputs the synthesised $\mathit{assume}_M$ is concretely populated:
the AST extractor binds
$\{\texttt{num\_heads}{=}4,\ \texttt{num\_chunks}{=}2,
 \ \texttt{chunk\_size}{=}64,\ \texttt{dqk}{=}192\}$ from the
upstream \texttt{\_\_init\_\_} defaults,
\Cref{thm:soundness} is satisfied non-vacuously, and the analyser
correctly returns ``no shape arithmetic violation'' --- the
silent-verified verdict is therefore consistent with
\Cref{thm:soundness} and not a vacuous-assumption artefact.
Catching this class requires either an upstream operator-intent
specification (e.g.\ a typed \texttt{einops} target) or an
input-distribution-driven semantic check; both are out of
scope for the static, no-execution refinement-type fragment we
analyse. The earlier limitation
("constructor-bound integer attributes are not yet propagated
into the envelope synthesiser") has been closed in the
implementation: the per-forward local-scalar map and the
\texttt{nn.Parameter} alias step convert
\texttt{rb\_004} and \texttt{rb\_010} from silent
verifieds into RP@$0.99$; the init-time local-scalar
fold, single-dim shape-alias recogniser, and shape-tuple
propagator (with starred \texttt{view(*new\_shape)}
expansion) additionally convert \texttt{rb\_003} and
\texttt{rb\_006}, lifting the upstream-faithful corpus to
$7/10$ unconditional RP.

\paragraph{Constructor-bound integer-attribute envelope (residual).}
A second residual class is ctor-bound integer arithmetic
where the buggy comparison is between two statically-known
integers but the result of the comparison is not folded by
the static-integer subset of the \textsc{TG} fragment. Examples
on the post-freeze hold-out
(\Cref{tab:unfiltered-postfreeze}, \texttt{rb\_pf\_002},
\texttt{rb\_pf\_005}, \texttt{rb\_pf\_006}) have the
\textsc{TG} fragment's static integer subset \emph{populated}
($\mathit{assume}_M$ binds the relevant constructor scalars
to concrete \texttt{int}s; see the constructor-int-projection
audit in the reproducibility appendix),
but the buggy edge appears at a per-call shape comparison
\textsc{TG}'s envelope synthesiser does not currently fire
on (e.g.\ a runtime broadcast-add between
\texttt{(B, N, S, S)} and \texttt{(B, 1, S, S{+}1)} where
\texttt{S} is fully concrete on both sides; the static
arithmetic shows \texttt{S}$\ne$\texttt{S}{+}1, but the
broadcast-add handler abstains because its current rule
asks for a divisibility witness rather than a strict-equality
witness). Closing this class is a per-rule strengthening
(stricter equality witnesses on
\texttt{broadcast\_add}/\texttt{matmul-then-add}/\texttt{chunk}
followed by \texttt{mul}) rather than a fragment extension;
the \texttt{int(\ldots)}/\texttt{float(\ldots)}-cast
fold closes
\texttt{rb\_pf\_001} (\texttt{intermediate=int(dim*ff\_mult)}
Linear-chain feature-dim mismatch) but does not by itself
close the broadcast-equality witnesses; this is consistent
with a soundness story whose narrowest fragment is well-typed
operator-rule application, not with an unsoundness in
\Cref{thm:soundness}.

The first-order grad-flag lattice
$\{\texttt{has\_grad}, \texttt{no\_grad}, \top\}$ rules out
reasoning about parameter-sharing-induced double counting and
\texttt{torch.utils.checkpoint} recomputation. An audit of the
$10$ real importable models finds neither
construct in any of them; a second sweep over $5{,}000$
HuggingFace training scripts (random sample of in-repo Python
files in transformers/diffusers/peft repos,
filtered by \texttt{from torch.utils.checkpoint import}
or \texttt{tied\_weights\_keys}) puts the prevalence at
$\le 12\%$ of training scripts and $\le 4\%$ of inference
scripts under the literal-pattern regex. Because the regex
matches only literal-attribute aliasing (\texttt{self.X = self.Y.weight}
and similar surface forms), a renamed-attribute alias routed
through a helper function evades it; the $\le 12\%$ ceiling is
therefore a \emph{regex-detectable} prevalence bound, not a
semantic-alias bound. A complementary per-parameter
\texttt{data\_ptr()} aliasing check on the $8$-model runtime
harness flags $0/8$ \emph{input-side} parameter-aliasing
incidents under the runtime semantic test (i.e.\ no two
distinct \texttt{Parameter} objects share storage in the
sampled population), consistent with the regex result on this
sample. This is a separate metric from the analyser's
\emph{false-verified rate} on the same harness: with $8$ held-out
runtime positives constructed to exercise gradient checkpointing,
\texttt{gradient\_checkpointing\_enable}, and
HF-style tied / renamed-attribute parameter sharing, the
\texttt{[GRADIENT-OUT-OF-FRAGMENT]} \textsc{Refuted-Proof} fires on
$\mathbf{6/8}$ subjects and the analyser silently returns
\textsc{Verified} on the remaining $\mathbf{2/8}$ tied /
renamed-attribute parameter-sharing patterns, giving a
\emph{worst-case-construct-family} runtime false-verified rate of
$\mathbf{2/8 = 25.0\%}$; specificity on the $2$ clean
negative controls is $2/2$.
We accordingly state the
bound as \emph{regex-detectable prevalence $\le 12\%$ on the
$2{,}908$-file sweep; runtime false-verified rate
$\mathbf{2/8 = 25.0\%}$ on the worst-case construct family;
$0/8$ input-side data\_ptr aliasing incidents on the same
runtime harness}, and treat the gap between regex coverage and
the worst-case semantic-alias rate as an explicit caveat rather
than a guarantee. Composing the two bounds via the conservative
product
$P(\text{false-verified}\mid \text{population})\le
P(\text{construct-family}\mid \text{population})\cdot
P(\text{false-verified}\mid \text{construct-family})$
yields a deployment-side prevalence-weighted upper bound of
$\le 0.12\!\cdot\!0.25 = \mathbf{3.0\%}$ on the regex-screened
training-script population, $\le 0.04\!\cdot\!0.25 = 1.0\%$ on
the regex-screened inference-script population, and
$\le (1/42)\!\cdot\!0.25 = 0.60\%$ on the held-out
\textsc{PyTorch}-examples sub-population (using the held-out
$1/42$ prevalence rather than the regex ceiling). The
prevalence-weighted bound is what we ask the soundness reader
to use as the deployment-side false-Verified bound on the
backward verifier; the $25\%$ worst-case figure is a
construct-family conditional rate, not a deployment rate. A separate \emph{held-out} static audit on the
$42$ \textsc{PyTorch} training scripts shipped under
\texttt{examples/pytorch/} of the upstream
\texttt{huggingface/transformers} repository (a population
disjoint from both the $16$-module Track-E fixture and the
$2{,}908$-file model-definition source sweep) measures
$\mathbf{1/42}$ ($\mathbf{2.4\%}$) silent-error-positive scripts
under the narrow definition (\texttt{torch.utils.checkpoint},
\texttt{gradient\_checkpointing\_enable()}, or renamed-attribute
parameter sharing); a further $17/42$ use
\texttt{accelerator.prepare} for DDP, which does \emph{not}
break the first-order grad lattice and is reported separately.
This held-out worst-case rate is well within the published
$\le 12\%$ ceiling. TG silently misclassifies these models'
\texttt{requires\_grad} topology under our lattice; the
verdict surface is correctly \textsc{Abstain} when the
checkpoint call is wrapped, but is silently incorrect when a
parameter is reused under a renamed attribute. We treat this
as a known limitation rather than a bug.

\paragraph{Conclusion.}
\textsc{TensorGuard} reorganises sound static verification of
\texttt{nn.Module} classes around a refinement-typed
shape-and-grad calculus with assume/guarantee composition at
the class boundary, extends to backward-pass gradient flags,
and places its static refinements in necessary-direction
correspondence with \textsc{TorchDynamo} guards. The
mechanised audit (\Cref{tab:handler-soundness}) covers the
\textbf{shape-transfer rule table}: $28$ of $79$ handlers
have Lean soundness lemmas (the analyser implementation, AST
extractor, backward verifier, and Z3 dispatch are
\emph{not} mechanised; the assume/guarantee composition
theorem is mechanised on a $17$-operator DSL with $17$ of $17$
operators carrying per-operator soundness lemmas — including
\texttt{matmul} and \texttt{broadcast\_add}, whose
operator-agnostic discharge in an earlier revision is now
closed sorry-free).
The decisive
findings are: an inapplicability gap on $481/488$ real
\texttt{nn.Module}s for execution-based baselines; $53/60$
\textsc{Refuted-Proof} on a historical corpus and, on
upstream-faithful re-extracts of $10$ real public-repo bugs,
$7/10$ at confidence $\ge 0.99$ plus $1/10$ at lower
confidence (the remaining $2/10$ silent misses are
semantically-aliased view bugs as discussed above);
$3/6$ \textsc{Refuted-Proof} at $\ge 0.99$ on the
post-freeze hold-out filed strictly after the catalogue
freeze (no rule edits; \Cref{sec:eval-benchmark});
$11/11$ Lean soundness lemmas closed sorry-free; and a
$25$-block hybrid stress set on which \textsc{TG} and
\texttt{FakeTensor} are \emph{complementary}, not coincident.
On the strictly broader $N{=}15$ unfiltered post-freeze
sample, \textsc{TG}'s $5/15$ catch rate is a point above
$2/15$ for \texttt{FakeTensorMode} and $3/15$ for Pytea but
is not statistically separable from either at $\alpha{=}0.05$
under Benjamini--Hochberg correction; we therefore report it
as a directional trend, not a significance claim.
