\section{Related Work}\label{sec:related}

\textsc{TensorGuard} sits at the intersection of refinement
types, abstract interpretation for ML, and the PyTorch shape
ecosystem.

\paragraph{Refinement and dependent types.} Liquid
Haskell~\citep{vazou2014liquidhaskell}, F$^\star$~\citep{swamy2016dependent},
Dafny~\citep{leino2010dafny}, Lean~\citep{moura2021lean4},
Dependent Haskell~\citep{eisenberg2016dependent}, and
\texttt{singletons}~\citep{eisenberg2012singletons} establish
the SMT-decidable / dependent-typing line; Hasktorch applied
type-level naturals to deep learning. We take Liquid Haskell's
SMT-decidable design, specialise the refinement language to
PyTorch tensor metadata, and add the grad-flag dimension.

\paragraph{Abstract interpretation and shape verifiers for ML.}
Ariadne~\citep{dolby2018ariadne} pioneered abstract shape
inference for TensorFlow; DeepPoly~\citep{singh2019deeppoly}
and AI$^2$~\citep{gehr2018ai2} certify robustness on
\emph{values} assuming shapes are correct;
\texttt{jaxtyping}/\texttt{torchtyping} are runtime checkers.
\textsc{TG} differs by symbolic, ahead-of-tracer shape reasoning
that additionally models \texttt{requires\_grad}.

\paragraph{PyTorch- and autograd-specific tooling.}
\texttt{FakeTensorMode}~\citep{faketensor} and
\texttt{torch.export}~\citep{torchexport} symbolically execute
graphs with metadata-only tensors but \emph{require the program
to run under a tracer} and do not reason about
\texttt{requires\_grad} flow across module boundaries.
TorchDynamo~\citep{ansel2024pytorch2} installs runtime guards;
\textsc{TensorGuard}'s contracts are the static refinement-type
analog (one-directional, \Cref{thm:dynamo-corr}). Pytea~\citep{lee2020pytea}
is the closest comparator in the no-execution / Z3 design
space; we evaluate it on both corpora
(\Cref{tab:headline}). Pytea targets shape errors only,
lacks \texttt{requires\_grad} reasoning, has no Lean
operator-rule audit, and is not designed around \textsc{Abstain}
as a first-class verdict; on the bug corpus it refutes $27/60$
versus \textsc{TG}'s $53/60$ and on the block corpus it is
\textsc{N/A} on $435/488$ for the same constructor-inapplicability
reason as \texttt{torch.fx}.

\paragraph{Assume/guarantee and contracts.}
Jones~\citep{jones1983tentative},
Meyer~\citep{meyer1992applying}, and
Findler--Felleisen~\citep{findler2002contracts} established
rely/guarantee, Design-by-Contract, and contract boundaries in
functional languages. We import these with shape/grad
refinements as the contract language at the
\texttt{nn.Module} class boundary. A nine-axis comparison is in
\Cref{tab:contrib}.
