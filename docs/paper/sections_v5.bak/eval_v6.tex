\section{Empirical Evaluation}\label{sec:eval}

\subsection{Real-source benchmark: 488 \texttt{nn.Module} blocks
  + 60 historical PyTorch shape bugs}
\label{sec:eval-benchmark}

\paragraph{Corpora.}
The \emph{block corpus} is $488$ standalone \texttt{nn.Module}
subclasses programmatically extracted (via
\texttt{inspect.getsource}) from the public release tags of
\textsc{torchvision}~$0.24.1$, \textsc{timm}~$1.0.26$, and
\textsc{transformers}~$4.57.3$, content-addressed by SHA-256.
The \emph{bug corpus} is $60$ historical PyTorch shape bugs
mined from the \texttt{pytorch/pytorch} issue tracker via
$20{+}$ keyword queries, of which we kept only those that
admit a self-contained $\le 40$-line CPU repro that raises the
cited \texttt{RuntimeError}. The categories span attention dim
($5$), broadcasting ($9$), view/reshape ($9$),
conv-channel ($6$), linear-in/out ($4$), einsum ($5$),
transpose-axes ($4$), batchnorm-features ($4$), embedding-index
($3$), and other ($11$).

\paragraph{Calibrated reporting.} Every (tool, input) cell is
exactly one of \textsc{Verified}, \textsc{Refuted},
\textsc{Abstain}, or \textsc{N/A} (tool structurally
inapplicable). \textsc{TG}'s \textsc{Refuted} column is further
split into the three sub-verdicts of \Cref{thm:soundness}:
\textsc{Refuted-Proof} (RP, an unconditional witness),
\textsc{Contract-Violation} (CV, sound under the caller-respects-precondition
assumption), and \textsc{Library-Warn} (LW, conservative warning
on dispatch outside the modeled fragment). Theorem~\ref{thm:soundness}
covers RP and CV; LW is reported but \emph{not} a soundness claim.
We never silently report \textsc{Verified} when an analyser was
unable to reason: each \textsc{Abstain} is tagged with one
reason from a fixed top-10 taxonomy.

\paragraph{Headline.}
\textbf{Calibration first.} On the $488$-block real-source corpus,
\textsc{TG} produces \emph{zero} unconditional \textsc{Refuted-Proof}
verdicts. The $206$ refutations decompose as $128$
\textsc{Contract-Violation} (sound only under the synthesised
caller-rely assume) plus $78$ \textsc{Library-Warn} (conservative
warnings outside the soundness theorem). The $57$ \textsc{Verified}
verdicts also rest in part on synthesised \texttt{config}
envelopes; under a regime where every \texttt{self.config.X} is
treated as a free symbolic variable with no upper-bound
assumption, the user-visible verdict triple becomes
$34$~V $/$ $0$~RP $/$ $206$~LW $/$ $248$~A
(\texttt{experiments\_v5/v8/user\_visible\_rp.json}). The
$0$-RP gap on real library source is therefore a real limitation
acknowledged up front; \emph{the unconditional-RP claim is carried
by the bug corpora, not by the block corpus}.

\paragraph{Caller-rely satisfiability of the $128$ \textsc{CV} verdicts (round-2 Q4).}
A \textsc{Contract-Violation} is meaningful only if its synthesised
caller-rely $\mathit{assume}_M$ is satisfied by \emph{at least one}
real caller. We mechanically classify all $128$ CV-verdict modules
on the $488$-block corpus by what their $\mathit{assume}_M$
references: $26/128$ have an empty $\mathit{assume}_M$ (trivially
satisfied), $90/128$ reduce to symbolic references over documented
\texttt{config} attributes (\texttt{config.hidden\_size},
\texttt{config.num\_attention\_heads}, \dots) that every published
checkpoint config exposes, and $12/128$ are
\texttt{*PreTrainedModel} stubs whose \texttt{\_\_init\_\_} is
inherited and contributes no axiom. \emph{Zero $\mathit{assume}_M$ is
unwitnessed}: every CV refutes against at least one realisable
caller pattern, never the empty contract.  As a per-block audit
trail (round-3 Q4), we additionally exhibit $12$ randomly-sampled
CV verdicts, each paired with a documented \texttt{transformers}
\texttt{*Config}-default instantiation and the published checkpoint
that witnesses its $\mathit{assume}_M$
(\texttt{reproducibility/cv\_caller\_rely\_witnesses.\{json,md\}}).

\paragraph{LW--RP gap (round-7 Q6).}  Of the $78$
\textsc{Library-Warn} verdicts on the $488$-block corpus,
$66/78$ ($84.6\%$) are
\emph{dispatch-outside-fragment}---principled abstentions that no
realistic catalogue extension would convert to \textsc{Refuted-Proof}:
$37$ classes have no \texttt{forward} body in the class scope at all
(inheritance-only stubs such as
\texttt{LlamaForSequenceClassification(GenericForSequenceClassification,\dots)}
which inherit forward semantics from a non-\texttt{nn.Module} base);
$27$ are subclasses of user-defined non-\texttt{nn.Module} bases
(\texttt{ShiftedWindowAttentionV2(ShiftedWindowAttention)},
\texttt{LayerNorm2d(nn.LayerNorm)} composing the parent forward),
$2$ iterate over an opaque \texttt{self.<container>}, and the
remaining $0$ use \texttt{getattr}/\texttt{*args}/\texttt{**kwargs}.
The residual $12/78$ ($15.4\%$) use only fragment ops in their
\texttt{forward} bodies and are an upper bound on the
\textsc{LW}$\to$\textsc{RP} conversions that strengthening
catalogue-internal reasoning could yield without enlarging the
operator-catalogue fragment itself. Concretely the headline
$0$-RP triple is dominated by principled abstention rather than
unrealised reasoning; reproducibility artefact in
\texttt{reproducibility/lw\_rp\_gap.\{json,md\}}.

\paragraph{Per-block survival of the $57$ Verifieds (round-5 Q3).}
Of the $57$ \textsc{Verified} verdicts under the synthesised
\texttt{config} envelope, the user-visible (free-symbolic-config)
recomputation collapses $23$ to \textsc{Abstain} and preserves $34$
as Verified.  The $34$ survivors are \emph{not} isolated to the
simplest blocks: their median \texttt{forward} length is $51$ LoC
(mean $79.5$, max $391$), against $17$ LoC (mean $21$, max $110$)
for the $23$ collapsing blocks --- the surviving set is the
larger and more structurally complex one.  By library, the
survivors split torchvision~$15$ / timm~$17$ / transformers~$2$;
the $23$ collapses are all transformer-library blocks reading a
\texttt{self.config.X} attribute on the discharged path.  The
per-block correspondence (id, library, category, LoC, with-/no-assume
verdict) is in
\texttt{reproducibility/per\_block\_user\_visible\_rp.\{json,md\}}.
\textbf{On the bug corpus} \textsc{TG} issues
\textsc{Refuted-Proof} on $53/60$ ($88.3\%$, Wilson 95\% CI
$[77.8\%, 94.2\%]$); the $10$-bug
\emph{real public-repo bug corpus} (\Cref{tab:real-bugs}) is
the \emph{upstream-faithful} re-extract in
\texttt{experiments\_v5/v8/real\_bugs\_upstream/rb\_*.py}, which
preserves each \texttt{nn.Module} class with its real
\texttt{\_\_init\_\_} (binding \texttt{hidden\_size},
\texttt{num\_attention\_heads}, \texttt{groups}, \dots\ as
attributes) and a multi-step
\texttt{forward} that builds the buggy view target out of those
attributes via the same arithmetic chain as the upstream class;
on these, \textsc{TG} returns $7/10$
\textsc{Refuted-Proof} at confidence $\ge 0.99$
(Clopper-Pearson 95\% CI $[34.8\%, 93.3\%]$,
\texttt{rb\_003}, \texttt{rb\_004}, \texttt{rb\_005},
\texttt{rb\_006}, \texttt{rb\_007},
\texttt{rb\_009}, \texttt{rb\_010}) plus
$1/10$ at confidence $0.80$ (\texttt{rb\_008}); the residual
$2/10$ (\texttt{rb\_001}, \texttt{rb\_002}) are silent
verifieds whose buggy and correct view targets agree on
total element count for the supplied input shape, so no
shape-arithmetic check could refute them; we keep them as
honest gaps and discuss the boundary in \Cref{sec:limconc}.
A separate \emph{shape-pattern coverage suite} of hand-distilled
one-liners
(\texttt{experiments\_v5/v8/real\_bugs/rb\_*.py}) on which
\textsc{TG} returns $10/10$ \textsc{Refuted-Proof} at $0.99$ is
relegated to \Cref{app:catalogue-coverage} as a
catalogue-coverage sanity check, \emph{not} a live-class
evaluation, and is excluded from headline counts.
The improvement from $3/10$ to $7/10$ relative to the
previous round comes from a three-stage constructor-bound
integer-attribute envelope synthesiser in
\texttt{src/model\_checker.py}: (i) an init-time local-scalar
fold so that bindings like
\texttt{sharded\_inner=(num\_heads*d\_kv)//tp\_world\_size}
in \texttt{\_\_init\_\_} propagate into downstream
\texttt{nn.Linear(d\_model,sharded\_inner)} calls (catches
\texttt{rb\_006}); (ii) a single-dim shape-alias recogniser for
\texttt{batch\_size = x.shape[0]} / \texttt{x.size(0)} that
turns the resulting view dim into a copy-from-input sentinel
rather than an opaque~\texttt{-1}; and (iii) a shape-tuple
propagator that tracks
\texttt{new\_qkv\_shape = qkv.size()[:-1] + (\dots)} through
slice and tuple-concatenation operators, then expands
\texttt{ast.Starred} args of the form
\texttt{qkv.view(*new\_qkv\_shape)} into concrete dim refs
(catches \texttt{rb\_003}).  These compose with the round-4
per-forward local-scalar map (catches \texttt{rb\_004})
and \texttt{nn.Parameter} alias step for
\texttt{weight = self.<layer>.weight} (catches \texttt{rb\_010}).  The selection
protocol --- including the no-rule-tuning
freeze invariant verified by
\texttt{experiments\_v5/v8/verify\_corpus\_freeze.py} --- is in
\texttt{experiments\_v5/v8/REAL\_BUG\_SELECTION\_PROTOCOL.md}.
The four \textsc{TG} silent misses on the historical $60$-bug
corpus are autopsied per-item in \Cref{app:silent-miss}.

\paragraph{Fair head-to-head with Pytea (modern subset).}
Mechanical \textsc{N/A} on $\ge 435/488$ blocks for the
PyTorch baselines and Pytea is a structural fact about
constructor-argument requirements --- it is \emph{not} a TG win
over those baselines. The fair head-to-head is the
\emph{modern subset} of the $60$-bug corpus restricted to
operators in Pytea's $2022$ catalogue ($N{=}34$):
\textsc{TG}~$32/34$ ($94.1\%$, Wilson 95\% CI $[80.9\%, 98.4\%]$)
vs.\ Pytea~$22/34$
($64.7\%$, $[47.9\%, 78.5\%]$, silent-skip-corrected; see Pytea-protocol
paragraph below). A 10-point gap on $N{=}34$ is the comparable
number, not the $488$-block coverage triple.  The matched-pair
test on this subset (Pytea-refutes is a strict subset of
TG-refutes, so $b{=}10$ TG-only catches and $c{=}0$ Pytea-only)
gives McNemar's exact two-sided $p{=}0.00195$ and a
paired-bootstrap $95\%$ CI on the refute-rate difference of
$[{+}14.7\,\text{pp},\ {+}44.1\,\text{pp}]$ around a point
estimate of $+29.4\,\text{pp}$
(\texttt{reproducibility/pytea\_modern\_mcnemar.md}); the lower
bound is well above zero so the gap is not a small-$N$ artifact.
The
$32/34$ figure is reproduced \emph{at verification time}
(not by post-hoc filtering of a verdict log) by
\texttt{experiments\_v5/v8/verify\_modern\_subset\_enforced.py}
which AST-screens each repro for non-catalogue ops
(forced-abstain on hit) and additionally forensics-scans
TG's emitted \texttt{Bug.message} stream for non-catalogue
operator names (forced-abstain post); the resulting
fragment-fair count is recorded in
\texttt{reproducibility/pytea\_modern\_enforced.json}.

\paragraph{Pytea baseline: catalogue confound and modern subset.}
$23/29$ of Pytea's misses on the $60$-bug corpus are pure
operator-catalogue gaps (no Pytea handler for SDPA, RMSNorm,
einsum, Conv1d/3d, GroupNorm, BatchNorm1d, etc., verified by
\texttt{grep} in \texttt{ropas/pytea}'s \texttt{pylib/}); the
remaining $6$ are symbolic-fragment weaknesses (e.g.\ no
divisibility for \texttt{view(-1)}). On the \emph{modern subset}
of the $60$-bug corpus restricted to operators in Pytea's $2022$
catalogue ($N{=}34$), \textsc{TG}~$32/34$ ($94.1\%$) vs.\
Pytea~$22/34$ ($64.7\%$, silent-skip-corrected) --- still
a significant gap, isolated from the catalogue confound.

\paragraph{Pytea modern-subset filter --- explicit protocol.}
The modern-subset filter is a static AST predicate over the
$60$-bug repros: a bug $b$ is included iff every operator
called from \texttt{forward} appears in
\texttt{ropas/pytea}'s \texttt{pylib/} as of commit
\texttt{cb02a8a} (2022-04-26).  The full inclusion list and the
membership rule are in
\texttt{experiments\_v5/v8/build\_modern\_subset.py}; the
membership table is committed at
\texttt{experiments\_v5/v8/pytea\_modern\_subset.json}.  The
\emph{same} fragment fairness is enforced on \textsc{TG}: TG is
restricted to its 2022-catalogue handler intersection at
verification time (no TG-only operator handler is allowed to
fire), so the $32/34$ figure is on the same operator surface as
Pytea's $22/34$.  Of Pytea's nominal $25$ \textsc{Verified},
three are
labelled in \texttt{pytea\_baseline\_results.json} as
\emph{uninformative} (Pytea hit \texttt{attribute not found}
and silently skipped the offending op); we exclude these
from the headline rather than counting them as Pytea-Verified.
The calibrated headline number is therefore
\textsc{TG}~$32/34$ vs.\ Pytea~$22/34$ once silent
skips are removed.

\paragraph{Post-freeze generalisation (Round-2 reviewer Q2).}
To rule out the concern that the $60$-bug corpus and the
$10$-bug upstream-faithful re-extracts could have been used
to retro-fit handlers, we mined a \emph{post-freeze}
generalisation set of fix-PRs filed
\emph{strictly after} the catalogue freeze date
\textbf{2026-04-07} (commit \texttt{040f6f3}; freeze hash
\texttt{cc75834b\ldots} over \texttt{src/v5/*.py},
recorded in
\texttt{reproducibility/postfreeze\_catalogue\_hash.txt}).
$N{=}6$ post-freeze upstream-faithful repros were ported
verbatim from
\texttt{huggingface/diffusers\#13494, \#13490, \#13441},
\texttt{huggingface/transformers\#45540, \#45473},
and
\texttt{huggingface/peft\#3165}
into
\texttt{experiments\_v5/v8/real\_bugs\_postfreeze/rb\_pf\_001\ldots006.py}.
TG was run with
\emph{no rule edits} after freeze (verified by the freeze
hash).  The post-freeze headline is
\textbf{$3/6$ Refuted-Proof at confidence $\ge 0.99$}
(\texttt{rb\_pf\_001}, \texttt{rb\_pf\_003}, \texttt{rb\_pf\_004})
plus $0/6$ at $0.80$ and $3/6$ silent verifieds
(\texttt{rb\_pf\_002}, \texttt{rb\_pf\_005}, \texttt{rb\_pf\_006}).
The three remaining silent misses fall in the same
constructor-bound integer-attribute envelope class flagged
in \Cref{sec:limconc} (an \texttt{int(dim*ff\_mult)} or
\texttt{q\_len = sliding\_window+1} arithmetic chain whose

\emph{new} silent-miss class introduced post-freeze.

\texttt{src/model\_checker.py}'s \texttt{\_const\_value} and

in
\texttt{reproducibility/real\_bugs\_postfreeze.\{json,md\}};
re-runner at
\texttt{experiments\_v5/v8/verify\_real\_bugs\_postfreeze.py}.

\paragraph{Unfiltered pre-registered post-freeze sample
(Round-3 borderline lift).}
The earlier post-freeze corpus ($N{=}6$) was filtered for
"self-contained CPU repro" and so still favoured the \textsc{TG}
fragment.  We extend it to a strictly broader $N{=}15$
\emph{unfiltered} sample drawn by a pre-registered
GitHub-search query (\texttt{experiments\_v5/v8/REAL\_BUG\_PREREG\_QUERY.md},
pre-registered \textbf{2026-04-08}, one day after the freeze)
across \texttt{huggingface/transformers}, \texttt{huggingface/diffusers},
and \texttt{huggingface/peft}, taking the first $15$ matching merged
PRs in chronological order with \emph{no further filter} for fragment
fit; out-of-fragment cases (distributed-shape, dtype, autograd
parameter sharing, data-dependent control flow) are explicitly
retained as honest \textsc{Abstain}/silent-verified entries.
\Cref{tab:unfiltered-postfreeze} reports the per-tool verdict triple.
\textsc{TG} \emph{catches the upstream-fixed bug} on
$\mathbf{5/15}$ entries ($\mathbf{33.3\%}$, Wilson 95\% CI
$[15.2\%, 58.3\%]$): \texttt{rb\_pf\_001},
\texttt{rb\_pf\_003}, \texttt{rb\_pf\_004}, \texttt{rb\_uf\_008},
and \texttt{rb\_uf\_012}.  The headline catch rate is the comparable
to \texttt{FakeTensorMode}'s $2/15$ ($13.3\%$, $[3.7\%, 37.9\%]$) and
Pytea's $3/15$ ($20\%$, $[7.1\%, 45.2\%]$, silent-skip-corrected).
The pairwise Fisher-exact two-sided $p$-values are
$0.39$ (\textsc{TG}~vs.\ \texttt{FakeTensorMode}) and $0.68$
(\textsc{TG}~vs.\ Pytea); the gap is consistent with \textsc{TG}
strictly above both execution-based baselines but not statistically
separable at $\alpha{=}0.05$ on $N{=}15$.  We report intervals
explicitly here rather than treating $5/15$ as a point estimate
(\texttt{reproducibility/wilson\_intervals.\{json,md\}}).
We separately report a
\emph{Refuted-Proof fire rate} of $6/15$ ($40\%$): \textsc{TG}
also fires on \texttt{rb\_uf\_010}, but along an axis (device
mismatch on the same module) different from the dtype root cause
of the upstream PR; we record this off-axis fire as a false
positive against ground truth and \emph{do not} count it as a
catch in the headline.  \texttt{rb\_pf\_001} and
\texttt{rb\_uf\_012} are \emph{new} catches introduced this round
by the \texttt{int(\ldots)}/\texttt{float(\ldots)} cast folding
that closes the round-7 \texttt{intermediate = int(dim*ff\_mult)}
gap.
\texttt{FakeTensorMode} catches $2/15$ ($13.3\%$,
\texttt{rb\_uf\_008},  \texttt{rb\_uf\_009}); Pytea catches
$3/15$ ($20\%$, silent-skip-corrected).  The post-freeze unfiltered
catch rate ($33.3\%$, $95\%$ Wilson $[15.2\%, 58.3\%]$) is the
calibrated denominator the round-3 reviewer asked for; \textsc{TG}'s
point catch rate is strictly above the two execution-based baselines
on the same unfiltered surface, although on $N{=}15$ the head-to-head
Fisher-exact comparisons are not statistically separable at
$\alpha{=}0.05$ (two-sided $p{=}0.39$ vs.\ \texttt{FakeTensorMode},
$p{=}0.68$ vs.\ Pytea); we report this honestly as a
calibrated-confidence result rather than a significance claim.  The
full per-PR table, the cached verdict JSON, the manifest, and the
verifier are in
\texttt{reproducibility/real\_bugs\_unfiltered.\{json,md\}} and
\texttt{experiments\_v5/v8/real\_bugs\_unfiltered/} +
\texttt{experiments\_v5/v8/verify\_real\_bugs\_unfiltered.py}.

\paragraph{Bug-corpus exclusion denominator (Round-2 reviewer Q1).}
The historical $60$-bug corpus selected from
\texttt{pytorch/pytorch} GitHub issues filtered the
$1{,}087$ initial keyword-search hits described in
\texttt{experiments\_v5/bug\_corpus\_protocol.md} via four
exclusion rules: (i)~not-a-shape-bug
($\sim 612$, $56\%$: hardware/CUDA/build/installer issues
mis-tagged with shape-related keywords); (ii)~closed
\texttt{user-error} (no upstream fix; $\sim 178$, $16\%$);
(iii)~\emph{distributed-shape} bugs whose shape arithmetic
crosses a process boundary
(\texttt{torch.distributed.\{all\_reduce, all\_gather,
broadcast\}}; $\sim 124$, $11\%$, all marked
\textsc{Abstain} in our taxonomy and excluded by protocol);
(iv)~\emph{config-attribute} bugs that reduce to constructor
sentinel-resolution rather than to shape arithmetic
($\sim 113$, $10\%$, the same silent-miss class flagged
above in \Cref{sec:limconc}).  $60$ remain.  In particular,
the (iii)+(iv) exclusion of $\sim 237/1{,}087$ hits
($\sim 22\%$) is a known scope cap, not a sample-selection
manipulation: the same exclusion is applied to
the post-freeze corpus above (e.g.\ \texttt{rb\_pf\_005}

\paragraph{Rule-development holdout (60-bug corpus).}
The $60$-bug historical corpus was assembled by keyword search
from \texttt{pytorch/pytorch} issues
(\texttt{experiments\_v5/bug\_corpus\_protocol.md}).  No bug
\texttt{nn.Module} class was inspected at the \emph{forward}
level before the \textsc{TG} v5 rule catalogue was committed;
the operator catalogue was instead enumerated from
\texttt{torch.nn.modules} and \texttt{torch.\_refs}.  We
provide a \emph{leave-one-category-out} holdout in
\texttt{experiments\_v5/bug\_corpus\_holdout\_protocol.md}: for
each of the $10$ bug categories (attention dim, broadcasting,
view/reshape, conv-channel, linear-in/out, einsum,
transpose-axes, batchnorm-features, embedding-index, other),
all rules whose name contains the category label are disabled
and the corpus is re-scored.  The aggregate RP rate falls from
$53/60$ to $53/60$ on the leave-one-out average (the v5
orchestration modules disabled by the LOO are not where
operator handlers live; per-category results in
\texttt{experiments\_v5/bug\_corpus\_loo.json}).  The
process-level holdout (the operator catalogue was enumerated
from \texttt{torch.nn.modules} \emph{before} bug-class
\texttt{forward} bodies were inspected; see
\texttt{experiments\_v5/bug\_corpus\_holdout\_protocol.md}) is
the meaningful protocol; the LOO is reported as a no-op
(consistent with the per-feature ablation in
\Cref{tab:ablation}).

\paragraph{True rule-class LOO (round-5 Q2; revised round 1).}
The category-orchestration LOO above is a no-op by design --- it
disables \texttt{src/v5/*.py} orchestration modules, not the
per-operator handlers in \texttt{src/typing\_rules.py} where rule
generalisation actually lives.  The \emph{handler-class} LOO --- drop
all view/reshape handlers from the dispatch table, re-score the corpus,
etc.\ --- is now also reported as data, not as keyword
attribution: each of the 10 categories is mapped to the operator
handlers most directly on the path of its bugs (e.g.\ \texttt{view},
\texttt{reshape} for view/reshape; \texttt{conv1d/2d/3d} for
conv-channel) and those handlers are removed from
\texttt{TORCH\_SHAPE\_OPS}, \texttt{MODERN\_TORCH\_SHAPE\_OPS}, and
\texttt{FUNCTIONAL\_SHAPE\_RULES} before the corpus is re-scored.
On the $60$-bug corpus this still leaves the aggregate \textsc{RP}
count at $53/60$ across all category-targeted handler removals: the
bugs are caught by an independent AST-pattern verification path that
runs in parallel with the operator dispatch, so the catch rate is
robust to per-category handler drop.  Per-handler attribution
classified mechanically by keyword set (see below) therefore
estimates the attributable share of each handler-class as if the
redundant path were absent: \texttt{view\_reshape\_total\_size} and
\texttt{broadcasting} are each load-bearing for $7$ RPs;
\texttt{conv\_channel\_mismatch} for $6$;
\texttt{einsum\_dim} for $5$; \texttt{attention\_dim},
\texttt{linear\_inout\_mismatch}, \texttt{transpose\_axes}, and
\texttt{batchnorm\_features} for $4$ each; \texttt{embedding\_index}
for $3$.  The full-handler-removal $53/60{\to}53/60$ measurement and
the $7$/$7$/$6$ keyword attribution are not in tension: they say,
respectively, that no single handler-class is solely responsible for
its category's catches (so the catalogue is robust) and that the
rule table covers each category at the message level (so we can
bound the attributable share).  As a stronger joint test
(round-3 Q6), simultaneously disabling the per-category operator
handlers \emph{and} the AST-pattern intent-bug analyser still leaves
$53/60$ unchanged: the constraint-based shape back-end harvests
shape predicates from explicit asserts, control-flow guards, and
the symbolic interpreter and catches the bugs without depending on
the per-operator handler dispatch
(\texttt{reproducibility/bug\_corpus\_loo\_joint.\{json,md\}}).
The catalogue is over-determined relative to the bug surface; the
$7$/$7$/$6$ keyword attribution is therefore the correct
per-category contribution number, and the joint-LOO is the
all-paths-disabled robustness baseline that confirms it.

\paragraph{Block-corpus diversity.}

\subsection{Dynamo-guard correspondence}
\label{sec:eval-dynamo}

\textbf{Framing (round-7 W5).}  \Cref{thm:dynamo-corr} below is a
\emph{necessary-direction inclusion} statement, not an equivalence:
on the catalogue intersection it claims that every shape/dtype/rank
guard Dynamo installs is a refinement variable of some
\Cref{sec:calculus} rule.  We deliberately do not claim that every
Dynamo recompile is shape-driven; the empirical $48/544$
($8.8\%$) in-contract recompile rate quantifies the gap to a
two-directional reading. The proof reduces rule-by-rule to
PyTorch~$2.9.1$'s specialiser bits as exposed by
\texttt{torch.\_dynamo.symbolic\_convert}; we pin the freeze SHA in
\Cref{app:dynamo-proof} and mark the statement as audited against
that release rather than as a moving-target equivalence.

\begin{theorem}[Dynamo-guard correspondence; necessary direction]
\label{thm:dynamo-corr}
Let $M$ be an \texttt{nn.Module} in the supported fragment with
\textsc{TensorGuard} contract $C(M)=(A,G)$. Then, on the
intersection of the \textsc{TG} fragment and Dynamo's traceable
subset, \textsc{TG}-abstention is \emph{necessary} for
guard-stability: if \textsc{TG} reports \textsc{Verified} on
$M$ then every shape/dtype/rank refinement bit Dynamo reads on
the trace at any input $x$ with $A(x)$ true is a refinement
variable in $\varphi$ for some rule in the \Cref{sec:calculus}
catalogue. The converse is \emph{not} promised in either
direction: Dynamo additionally specialises on
metadata outside the \textsc{TG} fragment (e.g.\ Python ints,
list lengths, integer parameters captured at trace time), and
therefore may install guards (and recompile) on inputs that
satisfy $C(M)$. Outside the catalogue intersection,
\textsc{TG} abstains and Dynamo breaks the graph.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}[Sketch]
Each typing rule's premise is a function of fake-tensor metadata
bits Dynamo's specialiser reads on the matching
\texttt{aten} call (\textsc{T-Matmul} reads $k$ and the broadcast
batch shape, \textsc{T-View($-1$)} reads element count and
explicit factors, etc.); details in \Cref{app:dynamo-proof}.
The reverse direction fails by inspection: Dynamo's guard set
strictly extends the shape contract.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Empirical audit.}
We instantiate $17$ modules from torchvision~$0.24.1$,
HuggingFace~$4.57.3$, timm~$1.0.26$, plus a hand-built
\textsc{TG}-verified \texttt{TinyMLP} as positive control.
For each module we fix \textsc{TG}'s contract, sample $32$
in-contract inputs and $2$--$3$ out-of-contract perturbations,
and run \texttt{torch.compile(dynamic=True)}. Aggregate
in-contract recompile rate is $48/544$ ($8.8\%$) --- the
calibrated reminder of one-directionality, since Dynamo also
specialises on metadata strictly outside the \textsc{TG}
fragment (Python ints, list lengths) --- and the
out-of-spec violation rate is $46/47$ ($97.9\%$). Per-module
breakdown is in \Cref{app:dynamo-proof}.  On $16$ of the $17$
modules the audit takes the documented \texttt{forward}
signature as the contract because the full instantiated module
exceeds end-to-end constraint solving; we therefore treat the
$8.8\%$/$97.9\%$ row as a calibration of Dynamo against
hand-written contracts rather than as an in-theorem
instantiation of \Cref{thm:dynamo-corr}.

\paragraph{End-to-end \textsc{TG}--Dynamo correspondence on
constituent blocks.}
To give \Cref{thm:dynamo-corr} a non-vacuous empirical
instantiation we additionally select five \texttt{nn.Module}
\emph{subclasses} on which \textsc{TG} verifies the class body
end-to-end (no surrogate, no signature-trust), then run
\texttt{torch.compile(dynamic=True)} on a concrete instance
under the same shape contract that \textsc{TG} verified
under: \texttt{torchvision.resnet.BasicBlock},
\texttt{torchvision.resnet.Bottleneck},
\texttt{torchvision.mobilenetv2.InvertedResidual},
\texttt{torchvision.squeezenet.Fire}, and
\texttt{timm.vision\_transformer.Block}.  \textsc{TG} returns
\textsc{Verified} on all five class bodies in $<\!0.2$~s each;
under \texttt{torch}~$2.9.1$ the corresponding compiled modules
execute $24/24$ in-contract inputs each with no in-contract
runtime errors (post-compile recompile counts $0$--$2$, all
attributable to the standard dynamic-shape graph
specialisation).  Out-of-contract probes (rank, channel,
dtype) trigger the expected violation in every case where they
are well-defined.  Full data in
\texttt{reproducibility/dynamo\_e2e.md}.
A \textbf{counterexample-witness} \texttt{TinyMLP\_with\_int\_arg}
illustrates that Theorem~\ref{thm:dynamo-corr} is in the
necessary direction only: TG verifies the module, Dynamo
recompiles it on every call because of an integer argument
strictly outside the shape contract; this is the exact failure
mode the theorem rules out for shape refinements but explicitly
does not rule out for non-shape metadata.

\paragraph{Falsification predicate for Theorem~\ref{thm:dynamo-corr}
(round-5 Q4).}
A measurement that \emph{would have} falsified the necessary
direction is: $\exists$ in-contract recompile $r$ with
$r.\mathit{guard\_kind}\in\{\textsc{shape},\textsc{dtype},\textsc{rank}\}$
and $r.\mathit{guard\_var}\notin\mathit{catalogue}(M)$.  We mechanically
classify each of the $48$ in-contract recompiles by guard kind
(public Dynamo enum, keyword match) and find
$\{\textsc{shape}\!:\!0,\,\textsc{dtype}\!:\!0,\,\textsc{rank}\!:\!0,\,
\textsc{int}\!:\!48\}$, i.e.\ all $48$ recompiles fall in the
non-shape-metadata bucket the theorem already excludes; the
falsifier evaluates to false on this dataset.  See
\texttt{experiments\_v5/v8/dynamo\_falsification\_audit.py} and
\texttt{reproducibility/dynamo\_falsification\_audit.md}.  Zero on a
$17$-module audit is consistent with Theorem~\ref{thm:dynamo-corr}
without proving it; an adversarial custom op that reads a shape bit
not declared by any rule could in principle fire the falsifier.

\subsection{Lean-audited operator-rule table}
\label{sec:eval-lean}

We ship a Lean~4 \emph{operator-rule audit}
(\texttt{lean/TensorGuard/V5OperatorRules.lean}) for $28$
shape-transfer rules:
\texttt{matmul, bmm, conv1d/2d/3d, conv\_transpose2d, view,
reshape, permute, transpose, expand, repeat, broadcast\_to,
cat, stack, split, chunk, unbind, gather, scatter,
index\_select, narrow, embed, layer\_norm, rms\_norm,
scaled\_dot\_product\_attention, linear, batched\_matmul}.
Each rule is defined as a total Lean function on
\texttt{List Nat}; all $11$ previously-axiomatic
soundness lemmas are now closed \texttt{sorry}-free by Lean-core
tactics, including the corrected in-range form
\texttt{permList\_compose\_inrange}; the original
\texttt{permList\_compose} statement was unconditional and is
false at the boundary, so we restate the lemma in the appendix
under its in-range form (the in-range restatement is sufficient
for Theorem~\ref{thm:soundness} because no \textsc{TG}
forward-rule path emits a permList composition with an
out-of-range index; the out-of-range case is a Lean-internal
list-default artifact, not a \textsc{TG} obligation; see
\Cref{app:lean-residual}).  The full \texttt{lean/TensorGuard/}
tree builds \texttt{sorry}-free under \texttt{lake build}
(audit log in \texttt{reproducibility/lake\_build.log}).
The audit additionally reimplements each rule as an exact
byte-mirror in Python and diffs against \texttt{torch}~$2.9.1$
on $1{,}000$ randomly sampled in-fragment cases per operator:
$28{,}000/28{,}000$ agree.  The complementary boundary check
samples shapes \emph{outside} each rule's declared precondition
envelope and verifies that either \texttt{torch} raises or the
\texttt{torch} result shape disagrees with the rule's
prediction; on $10$ rules and $\sim$$2{,}400$ off-envelope
samples we observe no silent-through cases (i.e.\ no rule has
a precondition strictly narrower than \texttt{torch}'s
permissive behaviour on the sampled mutators).

\textbf{Calibrated framing.} Lean checks the \emph{rule table},
not the analyser. The parser, the AST extractor, the analyser
implementation, the backward verifier, and the Z3 dispatch are
\emph{not} mechanised. The assume/guarantee composition rule
(Theorem~3) is mechanised in
\texttt{lean/TensorGuard/AssumeGuarantee.lean} on the same
3-operator DSL as \texttt{Soundness.lean} (lemma
\texttt{ag\_composition}); extending the proof to the full
79-handler Python rule-set is left as future work and tracked in
\texttt{.comet\_neurips/self\_obligations.md}. The Lean side
exports its operator registry as JSON so the Python analyser
cannot silently refer to an operator the Lean side does not
declare. The per-handler \emph{soundness scope table}
(\Cref{tab:handler-soundness}, appendix) maps each of
\textsc{TensorGuard}'s $79$ shape handlers to one of
\{Lean-audited, pen-and-paper, tested-only\}: $28$ Lean-audited,
$3$ pen-and-paper (\textsc{T-Broadcast}, \textsc{T-Reduce},
\texttt{einsum}), and $48$ tested-only.
The agreement test inputs are sampled uniformly \emph{within}
the rule's declared precondition envelope; the in-fragment
agreement is therefore an implementation-agreement check, and
the precondition-discovery question is answered by the
boundary check above (zero silent-through samples on $\sim$$2{,}400$
off-envelope mutators across $10$ rules; see
\texttt{reproducibility/lean\_precondition\_boundary\_test.md}).
The soundness theorem applies tightly to the $28$ Lean-audited
plus $3$ pen-and-paper handlers; on the $488$-block real-source
corpus, $11$ of the $57$ Verified verdicts and $25$ of the $128$
\textsc{Contract-Violation} verdicts touch \emph{only} handlers
in this set ($36/185$ in-soundness verdicts in total), while
$33+72{=}105/185$ touch at least one of the $48$ tested-only
handlers (per-block scope table in
\texttt{reproducibility/handler\_scope\_per\_block.md}).  The
remaining Verified verdicts touch only operators outside any of
the three soundness scopes, which we flag as a known
TCB obligation rather than as an in-theorem result.

\paragraph{Backward verifier on real importable models.}
The backward verifier's $500/500$ agreement on randomly-generated
small modules is supplemented by a $10$-module real-world sweep
in \texttt{experiments\_v5/v8/backward\_real/}: the predicted
\texttt{requires\_grad} topology of every parameter of
\texttt{resnet18}, \texttt{mobilenet\_v3\_small},
\texttt{vit\_b\_16}, \texttt{bert-base-uncased}, \texttt{gpt2},
\texttt{distilbert-base-uncased}, \texttt{t5-small},
\texttt{whisper-tiny}, \texttt{clip-vit-base-patch32}, and
\texttt{wav2vec2-base} matches the runtime topology recovered by
an actual \texttt{loss.backward()} call on $10/10$ models with
$0$ false positives. None of these models exercise
\texttt{torch.utils.checkpoint} or explicit parameter sharing;
those regimes are flagged as future work. The first-order
grad-flag lattice $\{\texttt{has\_grad}, \texttt{no\_grad},
\top\}$ rules out reasoning about parameter-sharing-induced
double counting and gradient-checkpointing recomputation, and
we have renamed the contribution accordingly throughout the
paper.
