# Review: TensorGuard — Static Tensor Shape Verification via SMT Theory Combination

**Reviewer:** César Sánchez
**Persona:** Formal Verification and AI Researcher
**Date:** 2026-03-02

---

## Summary

TensorGuard presents a static verifier for PyTorch `nn.Module` subclasses that aims to catch tensor shape mismatches, broadcast incompatibilities, and device/phase errors at analysis time without requiring any user annotations. The tool encodes verification conditions over a 5-theory SMT product domain — Shape × Device × Phase × Stride × Permutation — and employs Z3's `UserPropagateBase` API to implement five domain-specific theory solvers. The system claims to combine these theories via the Tinelli-Zarba arrangement enumeration procedure, justified by a 1,587-line Lean 4 mechanization with 71 theorems and 0 `sorry` obligations. An IC3/PDR engine provides unbounded verification for symbolic dimensions, and a decidability characterization classifies 94.9% of operators as QF_LIA (polynomial-time decidable).

The paper makes ambitious claims that span multiple active research areas: theory combination for non-stably-infinite theories, IC3/PDR adaptation to non-standard domains, Lean 4 mechanization of SMT soundness arguments, and CEGAR-based shape contract discovery. The benchmark evaluation on 18 models shows precision and recall of 0.900, substantially outperforming TorchScript (F1=0.714) and mypy (F1=0.000). While the breadth of the contribution is impressive, several aspects warrant careful scrutiny — particularly the gap between the formal claims and what is actually mechanized, the soundness of the theory combination in the presence of the stride theory's multiplicative constraints, and the completeness properties of the IC3/PDR adaptation.

The project represents a genuinely interesting line of work that connects formal methods to a practical problem that affects virtually every deep learning practitioner. However, as a formal verification researcher, I find several aspects of the soundness argument insufficiently rigorous, and the relationship between the Lean mechanization and the actual Python implementation has significant gaps that undermine the claimed guarantees.

## Strengths

1. **Well-motivated problem with real impact.** Tensor shape errors are arguably the most common class of runtime errors in deep learning code, and existing tools (TorchScript, mypy, Pyright) provide essentially no protection. The choice to target `nn.Module` subclasses is pragmatic — this is where shapes are structurally constrained by layer definitions, making static analysis feasible. The zero-annotation design philosophy removes a major adoption barrier that has historically plagued formal verification tools for ML.

2. **Principled theory combination architecture.** The decomposition into five domain-specific theories with clean axiomatic specifications is well-structured. The broadcast theory's formal specification (Σ_broadcast with axioms A1–A6) is particularly clean, and the decision to use Z3's UserPropagator API for eager theory propagation within DPLL(T) is technically sound. The observation that Device and Phase are finite-domain theories requiring Tinelli-Zarba rather than standard Nelson-Oppen is correct and shows genuine understanding of the theory combination literature. The practical bound of S(k, min(k, n)) on arrangement enumeration count, with concrete estimates of 52 arrangements for device (k≤4, n=5) and 8 for phase (k≤2, n=2), demonstrates careful complexity analysis.

3. **Lean 4 mechanization goes beyond boilerplate.** The mechanization is not merely a skeleton — it proves genuinely non-trivial results. The broadcast soundness and completeness theorems (`broadcastDim_sound`, `broadcastDim_complete`) establish bidirectional equivalence between the Boolean checker and the semantic specification. The broadcast associativity proof (`broadcast_assoc`) via reduction to `nat_max_assoc` is mathematically clean. The non-tautological `UserPropagatorSpec` structure that separates semantic predicates from executable checkers is a meaningful improvement over the naively tautological `UserPropagator.sound` field (which was literally `isConsistent assignment → isConsistent assignment`). The documentation within the Lean file is exemplary — the TCB (Trusted Computing Base) documentation explicitly states what is and is not mechanized.

4. **Decidability characterization is technically careful.** The distinction between the QF_LIA fragment (94.9% of operators) and the NP-hard reshape fragment is correctly stated. The observation that concrete-symbolic multiplication reduces to QF_LIA while fully symbolic multiplication enters QF_NIA is precise. The reference to the SUBSET-PRODUCT reduction for the NP-hardness lower bound and the appeal to Matiyasevich's theorem for the undecidability of unbounded QF_NIA show appropriate mathematical sophistication.

5. **IC3/PDR adaptation addresses a real limitation of BMC.** Bounded model checking with concrete dimension values cannot guarantee safety for all possible input shapes. The IC3/PDR approach of maintaining frame sequences F_0, F_1, …, F_k that over-approximate reachable states at each depth, with UNSAT-core-based generalization, is a principled solution. The explicit acknowledgment that the procedure is incomplete for the nonlinear (reshape) fragment but complete for QF_LIA is honest and correct.

6. **Comprehensive experimental infrastructure.** The experiments directory contains an extraordinary number of evaluation scripts covering ablation studies, cross-solver validation (Z3 vs cvc5), scalability analysis, mutation testing, and comparisons with multiple baselines (TorchScript, mypy, Pyright, PyTEA, jaxtyping, LLM-based approaches). This level of experimental thoroughness is rare in formal verification papers and suggests the authors take empirical validation seriously.

7. **CEGAR-based guard harvesting is a nice contribution.** The shape contract discovery loop (Houdini-style predicate accumulation) that iteratively discovers shape predicates from counterexamples is well-designed. The predicate language (DIM_EQ, DIM_GT, DIM_GE, DIM_DIVISIBLE, DIM_MATCH, NDIM_EQ, SHAPE_EQ) is expressive enough to capture common implicit shape contracts while remaining in a decidable SMT fragment. The backward trace from failing step to input shape assumptions is reminiscent of weakest precondition computation.

## Weaknesses

1. **Critical gap between Lean mechanization and Python implementation.** The Lean mechanization proves properties of *abstract specifications* (e.g., `broadcastConsistent`, `deviceConsistent`), but the actual Python UserPropagator implementations are entirely unverified. The TCB documentation states this explicitly: "Individual UserPropagator callback implementations are NOT mechanized. The consistency predicates are abstract specifications; the Python implementations are trusted to satisfy them." This is a significant gap. The Python `BroadcastTheoryPlugin` in `broadcast_theory.py` contains ~400 lines of non-trivial code including dimension alignment, padding logic, and conflict clause generation. A single bug in this code would invalidate the soundness guarantee. The Lean proof shows that *if* the propagators correctly implement their specifications, *then* theory combination is sound — but the "if" is doing enormous work. This is not unusual in verified systems (the CompCert approach also trusts the OCaml extraction), but it should be acknowledged more prominently rather than claiming "0 sorry" as if this implies end-to-end verification.

2. **Tinelli-Zarba combination with Stride theory is underspecified.** The paper describes a 5-theory product: Shape × Device × Phase × Stride × Permutation. The Tinelli-Zarba procedure handles the non-stably-infinite Device and Phase theories. But the Stride theory introduces *multiplicative* constraints (stride[i] = product(shape[i+1:])), which are not in QF_LIA. The theory combination procedure requires that shared variables between theories have compatible sorts. The Stride theory shares variables with the Shape theory (they both reason about dimension values), but the Stride theory's multiplicative constraints place it outside the linear arithmetic fragment. The Lean mechanization does not address this — it models the theories as abstract `TheorySolver` instances with an opaque `isSatisfiable` predicate. The actual interaction between the Shape and Stride theories, particularly for operations like reshape where both theories are active, needs more careful treatment. Specifically: does the Nelson-Oppen requirement of *convexity* hold for the stride theory? If not, the combination procedure may be incomplete even for the "polynomial" fragment.

3. **IC3/PDR soundness argument has a subtle issue.** The IC3/PDR implementation claims that "each frame F_i is an over-approximation of reachable states at depth ≤ i." In standard IC3 for hardware model checking, frames are conjunctions of clauses over Boolean variables, and the over-approximation property is maintained by construction. In TensorGuard's adaptation, "states" are symbolic shape assignments (tensor_name → dimension tuple), and the state space is infinite (symbolic dimensions range over ℤ≥1). The key question is: how are cubes (ShapeCube) generalized after blocking? Standard IC3 uses UNSAT cores from the SAT solver, but here the solver is an SMT solver over QF_LIA. The documentation mentions "UNSAT-core-based generalization (via Craig interpolation when available)" — but Craig interpolation for QF_LIA produces quantifier-free interpolants that may not be expressible as cubes (conjunctions of literals over dimension variables). If the generalization step produces over-general blocking clauses, the frames may not be valid over-approximations, breaking soundness. This needs formal justification.

4. **The 117 operator transfer functions are tested, not proven.** The system models 117 of ~2000 PyTorch operators, and unsupported operators are treated as identity (the paper claims this is "conservative"). But treating an unknown operator as identity is *not* conservative in general — if an unsupported operator changes the shape (e.g., `torch.fft.rfft` halves the last dimension and adds a complex dimension), the identity assumption produces an *unsound* result. It is only conservative if the unsupported operator is shape-preserving, which is an unverified assumption. Furthermore, even the 117 modeled operators are validated by testing (the extensive test suite), not by proof. Given the complexity of some transfer functions (Conv3d, Transformer layers, PixelShuffle), testing alone cannot provide soundness guarantees. The Lean mechanization proves generic properties (broadcast is associative, stride formula is correct) but does not prove that the specific Python implementation of each transfer function correctly encodes the corresponding PyTorch semantics.

5. **CEGAR loop termination is not guaranteed.** The shape CEGAR implementation uses a bounded iteration budget, and the documentation states that "CEGAR iteration budget exhausted" produces a TIMEOUT verdict. But the more fundamental question is: under what conditions does the CEGAR loop converge? The predicate language has 7 kinds (DIM_EQ, DIM_GT, etc.), but the space of possible predicates over all tensors, axes, and dimension values is countably infinite. Unlike Houdini (which starts with a finite candidate set and only removes predicates), TensorGuard's CEGAR loop *adds* predicates from counterexamples. For convergence, one needs to show that the predicate lattice has finite ascending chains — i.e., that no infinite sequence of strictly finer predicates can be generated. This is claimed but not proven. In practice, non-convergence manifests as the TIMEOUT verdict, but the lack of convergence guarantees limits the theoretical contribution.

6. **Benchmark evaluation is too small to support the claims.** The headline comparison (TensorGuard vs TorchScript vs mypy) uses only 18 benchmarks (10 buggy, 8 correct). With such a small sample, the precision/recall numbers have wide confidence intervals. A single misclassification changes precision by 0.1. The paper should include confidence intervals or at minimum a sensitivity analysis. Moreover, the benchmark models appear to be synthetic (constructed to test specific bug patterns) rather than real-world models from production codebases. The extensive experiments directory contains many more evaluation scripts, but the headline results are from this tiny benchmark. The absence of standard ML models (ResNet, BERT, GPT-2) from the main evaluation is conspicuous.

7. **Lean file contains 3 occurrences of the word "sorry" in documentation comments.** While the claim is "0 sorry obligations" and indeed the word appears only in documentation strings (not as Lean tactics), the mechanization still has substantive gaps. The `combination_soundness` theorem proves that if an arrangement is jointly consistent, each theory has a model consistent with that arrangement — but it does *not* prove that the models can be *combined* into a single valuation satisfying all theories simultaneously. The standard Tinelli-Zarba result requires showing that the individual models agree on shared variables *and* can be extended to a combined model. The Lean proof sidesteps this by defining `combinedSatisfiable` as "each theory has a model consistent with the *same arrangement*" rather than "there exists a single model satisfying all theories." This is a weaker statement than the actual Tinelli-Zarba theorem, and the gap is not acknowledged.

## Minor Issues

- The `UserPropagator.sound` field in the original Lean structure is literally tautological (`isConsistent assignment → isConsistent assignment`). The paper acknowledges this and introduces `UserPropagatorSpec` as a fix, but the original definition remains in the file and could confuse readers.
- The `arrangement_count_bound` theorem proves only that the number of arrangements is at most n^k, which is a trivial upper bound. The claimed connection to Stirling numbers is stated in comments but not formalized.
- The paper claims IC3/PDR "completeness" for the QF_LIA fragment, but IC3 completeness requires that the transition relation is encodable as a single formula — is this the case when the computation graph has variable-length sequential composition?
- The `broadcastPropagatorSpec` uses `List.finRange` and `List.all_eq_true`, which depends on Lean's decidable equality instances. This is correct but relies on the Lean stdlib being trustworthy (explicitly acknowledged in the TCB).
- The integration between the CEGAR loop and IC3/PDR is unclear — can CEGAR-discovered predicates be used as IC3 frame clauses? If so, how is the interaction formalized?
- The `DeviceTheoryPlugin` supports only 5 devices (CPU + CUDA:0–3). Modern GPU clusters may have 8+ GPUs, and TPU/XPU devices are not modeled.
- Several experiment scripts import from `src.craig_interpolation`, but Craig interpolation for QF_LIA is only available through cvc5, not Z3. The fallback "simulated interpolant" approach is ad hoc.

## Questions for Authors

1. **On the Lean-to-Python gap:** Have you considered using extraction (Lean → code) or property-based testing (QuickCheck-style) to systematically validate that the Python UserPropagator implementations satisfy their Lean specifications? The current approach relies on unit tests, which provide weaker coverage guarantees.

2. **On theory combination with multiplication:** The Stride theory involves multiplication (stride[i] = product(shape[i+1:])). The Nelson-Oppen combination framework requires theories to be *signature-disjoint*. Since both Shape and Stride share the Dim sort and arithmetic operations, are they truly signature-disjoint? If not, how do you justify the combination? If they share a common sub-theory (e.g., linear arithmetic), you would need Shiny's extension of Nelson-Oppen for non-disjoint signatures.

3. **On IC3/PDR cube generalization:** When Z3 returns an UNSAT core for a blocked cube, how do you generalize it to a clause? Standard IC3 drops literals from the cube and re-checks. In your setting, literals are constraints over ℤ≥1 (e.g., dim_0 ≥ 1, dim_0 = 768). Dropping dim_0 = 768 produces an over-generalization. Do you use interpolation-based generalization, and if so, what guarantees does it provide for the QF_LIA fragment?

4. **On the "conservative" treatment of unsupported operators:** You claim unsupported operators are treated as identity, which you call conservative. But identity means shape-preserving. Is this not *optimistic* rather than conservative? A truly conservative approach would treat unsupported operators as producing an unknown shape (⊤ in the lattice), which would propagate uncertainty to downstream operations. Why did you choose identity over ⊤?

5. **On scaling to real models:** Have you attempted to verify standard architectures like ResNet-50 (which uses skip connections with shape-matching requirements), BERT (which involves complex attention shapes), or GPT-2 (which uses causal masking with specific dimension constraints)? If so, what was the experience? If not, what are the anticipated challenges?

## Recommendation

**Weak Accept.** The problem is important, the technical approach is principled, and the Lean mechanization is a genuine contribution to the verified SMT literature. However, the gap between the formal claims and the actual guarantees (particularly the Lean-to-Python disconnect, the incomplete theory combination for the Stride theory, and the IC3/PDR generalization issue) is concerning. The benchmark evaluation is too small to draw robust conclusions. I would recommend acceptance conditional on: (1) a clear separation of what is proven vs. trusted, (2) at least one real-world model (ResNet or BERT) in the evaluation, and (3) a formal statement of the assumptions under which the soundness guarantee holds, including the identity-for-unsupported-operators assumption.

**Confidence:** 4/5
