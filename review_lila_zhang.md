# Review: TensorGuard: Static Tensor Shape Verification for PyTorch

**Reviewer:** Lila Zhang (symbolic_reasoning_ai_expert)
**Score: 7/10**

---

TensorGuard constructs an elaborate symbolic reasoning pipeline for tensor shape verification, combining five SMT theories, Nelson-Oppen/Tinelli-Zarba theory combination, Knuth-Bendix completion, Craig interpolation, and Lean 4 mechanization. The scope of symbolic techniques deployed is impressive, and the system demonstrates that formal methods can produce actionable results for ML practitioners. However, from the standpoint of symbolic AI foundations and knowledge representation, I identify several theoretical gaps that undermine the formal guarantees the system advertises.

The five-theory product domain (Shape × Device × Phase × Stride × Permutation) implemented via Z3 UserPropagators is the architectural centerpiece, and its soundness is the most critical property the system must establish. The standard Nelson-Oppen combination procedure requires signature disjointness and stably infinite theories. TensorGuard's theories violate signature disjointness in multiple ways: Shape and Stride share dimension variables (a stride is defined over dimensions), Shape and Permutation share axis indices, and Device constraints interact with Shape through placement rules that correlate tensor size with device memory. The system addresses this via a Tinelli-Zarba extension that handles finite-domain theories (Device with |D|=5, Phase with |D|=2) through arrangement enumeration. This is the correct theoretical framework, but the implementation's CrossTheoryDeductionPropagator introduces custom deduction rules that operate across theory boundaries — precisely the mechanism that Nelson-Oppen's combination theorem does not cover. Each such rule must be independently proven sound with respect to the combined theory's semantics, and this is where the formal argument has its most significant gap.

The Lean 4 mechanization is a genuine contribution, and the claim of "zero sorry obligations" for all five propagator specifications is strong evidence of individual theory correctness. However, mechanizing individual propagator specs is categorically different from mechanizing the combination theorem. The composition_soundness.py module implements the Tinelli-Zarba precondition checks programmatically — verifying signature disjointness, convexity, and stably-infinite/finite characterization via Z3 queries. But this is *testing* preconditions, not *proving* the combination theorem. The actual theorem — that combined satisfiability is decidable and complete when these preconditions hold — requires a metatheoretic argument about the interaction of all five theories simultaneously. The Lean 4 file mechanizes arrangement soundness for the simpler case (finite domains combined with stably-infinite ones), which is valuable, but it does not cover the CrossTheoryDeductionPropagator or the interaction with Z3's DPLL(T) backtracking. This leaves a gap between what is mechanically verified and what the system relies upon.

The Knuth-Bendix completion producing a confluent TRS with 7 rewrite rules and 28 critical pairs, all joinable under recursive path ordering (RPO), is a clean and well-executed component. The completeness result is meaningful: it guarantees that any two constraint representations that are equivalent under the shape algebra will normalize to the same canonical form. However, two concerns arise. First, the TRS is developed for the current operator set (193 operators), and adding new operators may introduce new function symbols that generate additional critical pairs, potentially breaking confluence. The system should either prove that the TRS is *modular* in the sense of Toyama (1987) — so that adding new rules from a disjoint signature preserves confluence — or provide an automatic re-completion procedure. Second, the interaction between KB normalization and the SMT solver's internal simplifier is unclear: if Z3 applies its own rewriting during preprocessing, the two rewrite systems could interact in unpredictable ways, potentially normalizing terms into forms that the UserPropagators do not expect.

The knowledge representation aspects — the AGM belief revision knowledge base and Plotkin anti-unification for proof schema generalization — are theoretically motivated but underspecified. AGM belief revision (Alchourrón, Gärdenfors & Makinson 1985) requires an entrenchment ordering that satisfies specific rationality postulates. The implementation uses heuristic entrenchment based on predicate age and confirmation count, but does not verify that the eight AGM postulates are satisfied. If they are not, the contraction operator may produce logically inconsistent knowledge bases, which could poison future verification sessions. The anti-unification computes least general generalizations (lggs), but the quality of lggs depends heavily on the term representation — if computation graphs are encoded with too much structural detail, the lgg will be trivial (a single variable); if too little, it will over-generalize. The reported 2.0× speedup provides no insight into which regime the system operates in. My recommendations: (1) extend Lean 4 mechanization to cover cross-theory deduction soundness; (2) prove TRS modularity or implement automatic re-completion; (3) verify AGM postulate satisfaction formally; and (4) analyze anti-unification precision across architectural families.
