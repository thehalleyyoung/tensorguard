## Motivation

- **For what purpose was the dataset created?** To measure the accuracy of static bug detectors for PyTorch `nn.Module`s against a stable, executable ground truth that cannot silently drift. Each model is labeled `clean` (should verify **SAFE**) or `buggy` (should verify **UNSAFE**).
- **Who created it and how is it maintained?** The TensorGuard maintainers; it is versioned (`VERSION`), content-addressed by SHA-256 in `manifest.json`, and regenerated by `build_manifest.py`.

## Collection & labeling process

- **How were instances acquired?** Buggy models were distilled from real `pytorch/pytorch` issue reports and two canonical failure patterns; clean models are minimal, idiomatic architectures.
- **How are labels grounded (not the tool's opinion)?** Most buggy models raise a real `RuntimeError` when executed in eager PyTorch (the `expected_error_substring` field records the message); the device bug raises only on a CUDA host; the gradient-detach bug is *silent* (raises no exception), which is exactly why static verification matters. Clean models are validated to execute without error before admission.

## Uses

- **Intended use:** benchmarking precision/recall/F1 of static and runtime bug detectors (see `evaluation/precision_recall.py`, `evaluation/significance.py`).
- **Out-of-scope:** the corpus is intentionally small and curated; it is a *ground-truth correctness* benchmark, not a representative sample of all PyTorch code. Headline accuracy numbers are always reported with honest sample-size caveats (n = 16).
